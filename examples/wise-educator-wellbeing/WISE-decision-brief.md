# WISE Educator Wellbeing — Stage 1 Desk Research Brief
_A consultation + clinician-capacity layer around a tiered framework for K-12 educator wellbeing · Recommendation: CONDITIONAL GO · Likely path: spin-out if willingness-to-pay validates, else spin-in_

## Recommendation
**Conditional go** — advance to customer discovery, scoped tightly to the one question that decides this: will a non-federal buyer pay *recurring* dollars for educator wellbeing? The problem is large and real, the WISE asset is real and squarely on-mission, and Thrive's clinical-validation engine is decisive for the evidence gap. But there is **no dedicated funding for adult wellbeing**, the federal buckets that fund the adjacent work are being cut, and the field is **fragmented, not empty**. The single condition that flips this to a confident go: a foundation-seeded pilot that converts toward a district/state contract on a **retention + student-outcome** frame.

## The Hypothesis
_If we provide a Georgetown-grounded tiered framework plus implementation consultation and a trained clinician workforce, then districts and states will improve educator wellbeing, retention, and (downstream) student outcomes — enough to pay for it recurring._ The burning problem: educators are the most-burned-out U.S. workforce, and turnover costs up to ≈$25k per departure.

## Market Overview
**Market Size: 2/3** · **Revenue Potential: 2/3** — _Macro K-12 pond is huge, but the fundable adult-wellbeing slice is small, fragmented, and unsizable from public data; a revenue path exists (retention-framed contracts) but is speculative and comparable orgs are thin and grant-dependent._
- **Size:** TAM ≈$984B K-12 spend / ≈99k schools / ≈3.2M teachers [[NCES](https://nces.ed.gov/ccd/tables/202223_summary_2.asp); [Census FY24](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/school-system-finances.html)]; but no adult-specific SAM exists — every market number bundles *student* SEL. Honest SOM: low single-digit $M, below the $3–15M band absent the contract path.
- **Where the money comes from:** today, overwhelmingly student-tagged streams; adult wellbeing has no dedicated line. The funding *environment is deteriorating* (ESSER cliff; Title II-A/IV-A slated for elimination; ≈$1B school-MH grants cut to ≈$208M) — which hits the buyer's budget, inverting the brief's "collapsing funding forces innovation" framing.
- **Demand temperature:** concern is hot (41% of leaders worried about staff MH) but budget is cold — concern hasn't converted to a procurement category.

## Competitor Landscape
**Competitive Position: 2/3** — _"No scalable competitor" is false; the field is fragmented but populated, and the strongest causal evidence is held by a competitor (CARE), not WISE. Real whitespace exists but no defensible differentiation yet._
- **Direct:** The Teaching Well (best-funded, ≈$3.79M FY25); CARE/CREATE (the RCT + What Works Clearinghouse + CDC evidence WISE lacks); Educators Thriving (Harvard/AFT traction); FuelEd (≈91% revenue decline — a distress signal).
- **Adjacent / workarounds:** Panorama (real "adult SEL" + district data, the fast-follower threat); cheap EAPs (≈$14/head) and **free** apps (Headspace, Calm, Kaiser) own the low end. Pricing is near-universally quote-based; Georgetown's own IECMH certificate (≈$6k) is the cleanest credential-layer anchor.
- **"Do nothing":** strong — the pain is chronic, not acute, and a $14 EAP lets a district say it "did something." Note category-wide post-ESSER strain (Hazel, Second Step, acquisitions, Mindful Schools defunct).

## User / Payor / Referrer Map
- **User:** the classroom teacher and, pivotally, the building principal (Tier 1 is leader-driven).
- **Payor:** rarely the user — a funded foundation/intermediary first, then a district (PD/student-services champion, CFO/board signing), then a state.
- **Referrer:** the Georgetown/Thrive network and conference relationships; a foundation program officer who routes WISE into grantee districts.
- **First dollar:** the **foundation/intermediary pass-through** (Education Plus Health-style) is the most credible first check — but it's pilot capital, not recurring revenue (only ≈12% of foundation grants are multi-year; NoVo and CZI both exited this space). The durable payer is a district/state operating-budget contract, won on retention/outcome framing and made repeatable via a Sourcewell/state master agreement.

## UVP & Unique Advantages
The next best alternative is a cheap EAP + occasional PD day. WISE's verifiable difference is **clinical depth across tiers + Georgetown research credibility** — but that's verifiable to a clinician, not yet to a CFO, because the outcome (retention/student impact) isn't measured. Of Thrive's three advantages, **nimble clinical validation is decisive** (it builds the missing evidence that unlocks the sale); learning-partner pilots are strongly load-bearing. _For leaders losing teachers they can't afford to replace, WISE provides a clinically-grounded, Georgetown-backed tiered system that improves wellbeing and retention — unlike an EAP or one-off PD — because it pairs real mental-health consultation with research-designed proof._

## Open Questions & Riskiest Assumptions
The venture dies fastest if **willingness-to-pay for adult wellbeing isn't real or recurring** (no dedicated funding, adverse timing, free/commoditized low end), and next if **the retention/outcome causal evidence can't be produced**. Cheapest highest-leverage test: confirm the Education Plus Health relationship and get one foundation to commit a scoped pilot with a retention metric, then 3–5 district CFO/student-services discovery calls to find the real budget owner. Also correct the overstated claims (MA "statewide" is an RFI pursuit, not live; the SAMHSA tie is unverified; Megan is Director, not founder) and run a "WISE" naming/trademark clearance. _Full evidence, competitor profiles, and sourcing in the in-depth report._
