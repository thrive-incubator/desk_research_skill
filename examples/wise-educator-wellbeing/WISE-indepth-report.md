# Stage 1 Desk Research: WISE Educator Wellbeing

_A consultation and clinician-capacity-building layer around a tiered (MTSS-style) framework for K-12 educator wellbeing — for school districts, states, and foundations. Not a student SEL curriculum._

## Executive Summary

The problem is real, large, and well-documented; the asset is real; the mission fit is excellent; the **willingness-to-pay thesis is the weak link, and the timing is adverse.** WISE — the MedStar Georgetown Center for Wellbeing in School Environments — is a genuine, ≈decade-old program delivering tiered school mental health in ≈30 D.C. schools, led by a real clinical researcher (Dr. Megan Leigh McCormick). Its insight — that improving the *adult* workforce's wellbeing lifts school culture and, downstream, students — sits squarely inside Thrive's mission and is a clean neighbor to Attune/Attunify (K-12 educators vs. IECMH 0–5).

But the bet is that this can be a **sustainable company**, and the evidence cuts against the easy version of that story. There is **no dedicated, durable funding stream for *adult* educator wellbeing** anywhere in the system; the federal buckets that fund the adjacent work (Title II-A, Title IV-A, school mental-health grants, ESSER) are being frozen, cut, or eliminated in 2025–26; what districts already buy for adults is cheap and commoditized (≈$14/head EAPs) with free competitors (Headspace, Calm, Kaiser) owning the low end. The competitive field the brief calls empty is in fact **fragmented but populated** — The Teaching Well, CARE/CREATE (which has the RCT evidence WISE lacks), Educators Thriving, FuelEd — most of them small, grant-dependent, and quote-priced, with at least one (FuelEd) in visible financial decline. Several of the brief's self-claims do not survive the public record: the "statewide in Massachusetts" Tier-2 deployment (it is an RFI pursuit, not a live program), the "decade of research proving" the educator→culture→student chain (one dissertation plus internal evaluation), and the "SAMHSA-adjacent credibility halo" (unverified; "WISE" actually collides with *Classroom WISE*, a SAMHSA-funded educator-MH training in the same space).

This is not a kill. The problem is durable, the asset is real, and the path the category's survivors actually use — **lead with teacher-retention dollars and student outcomes, seed with a foundation/intermediary pilot, then migrate to recurring district/state contracts** — is open to WISE, and Thrive's clinical-validation engine is genuinely load-bearing for closing the evidence gap that would unlock it. But it is a conditional, prove-the-money case, not a fund-it-on-conviction case.

**Recommendation: CONDITIONAL GO** — advance to customer discovery, scoped narrowly to the one question that decides this: will a non-federal buyer pay real, *recurring* dollars (a foundation-seeded pilot converting toward a district/state contract, framed on retention + student outcomes)? **Likely path: spin-out if WTP validates; otherwise a strong spin-in (Center program) candidate rather than a standalone company.**

## 1. The Hypothesis & Why Now

### The hypothesis (If/Then)
_If we provide districts and states a tiered, Georgetown-grounded framework plus implementation consultation and a trained clinician workforce, then schools will improve educator wellbeing and organizational health — and retain more teachers and improve student outcomes — enough to pay for it on a recurring basis._ The buyer is a district or state (often reached first through a funded intermediary); the burning problem is educator burnout and turnover; the measurable change is retention and school-climate improvement.

### The problem (current data)
Educators are the most-burned-out workforce in the country. Gallup found K-12 workers had the highest burnout of any U.S. industry — **44% "always/very often" burned out**, and **52% of teachers** specifically [Gallup, 2022](https://news.gallup.com/poll/393500/workers-highest-burnout-rate.aspx). RAND's *State of the American Teacher* found teachers report roughly **2x** the frequent job-related stress of comparable working adults [RAND, 2024](https://www.rand.org/news/press/2024/06/18.html), and its 2025 follow-up found **fewer than half** of teachers say their school or district is making real efforts to support work-life balance [RAND, 2025](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA1108-20.html) — i.e., the need is unmet, which is the venture's opening. Turnover is the costly symptom: **15.1%** of public-school teachers moved or left between 2020–21 and 2021–22 [Learning Policy Institute, 2024](https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/teacher-turnover-united-states-factsheet), and **74%** of schools reported difficulty filling a vacancy entering 2024–25 [NCES, 2024](https://ies.ed.gov/learn/press-release/most-u-s-public-elementary-and-secondary-schools-faced-hiring-challenges-start-2024-25-academic-year).

### Cost of inaction (the buyer's frame)
This is the venture's strongest ROI hook. The most current cost-of-turnover estimate is **up to ≈$25,000 per departure** in large districts ($11,860 small / $16,450 medium / $24,930 large) [LPI, 2024](https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/2024-whats-cost-teacher-turnover). The old national aggregate ("$2.2 billion/year") is from a **2014** report and should not be presented as current [All4Ed, 2014](https://all4ed.org/press_release/teacher-attrition-costs-united-states-up-to-2-2-billion-annually-says-new-alliance-report/); no authoritative post-2020 national aggregate exists — a gap to note. The buyer's frame should therefore be per-departure dollars saved, not a stale national number.

### Research base for the thesis
Here the brief overstates. The educator→culture→student causal chain is a genuine *research interest* of the clinical lead [Georgetown faculty profile](https://psychiatry.georgetown.edu/megan-leigh-mccormick-ph-d/), but the visible published record specific to WISE is thin: the most direct item is a **2024 doctoral dissertation** evaluating the WISE Educator Therapy Program (qualitative, n=10, not peer-reviewed) [ERIC ED657704, 2024](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED657704), plus internal program evaluation in the 2023 annual report. Independently, the strongest *causal* evidence in this category belongs to a competitor, not WISE: the CARE program's RCT (36 urban schools, 224 teachers; significant reductions in psychological distress) in the *Journal of Educational Psychology* [Jennings et al., 2017](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1158314). Treat WISE's evidence as practitioner experience + one dissertation, and treat *building* the evidence base as a core Stage-2/3 workstream (and a place Thrive's research engine is decisive).

### Why now — the inflection
The brief frames "collapsing federal funding forces innovation" as a tailwind. **The research inverts this:** the collapse hits the *buyer's* budget. The ESSER cliff (obligation deadline Sept 30, 2024; liquidation extensions rescinded March 2025 [Edunomics Lab, 2025](https://edunomicslab.org/2025/03/26/the-esser-hangover-is-here/)) removed the one-time money that funded 76% of districts' mental-health initiatives [AASA, 2024](https://www.aasa.org/news-media/news/2024/09/09/aasa-publishes-final-arp-district-spending-survey-report). Title II-A ($2.19B) and Title IV-A ($1.38B) were both slated for elimination in the FY2026 block-grant proposal (Senate Appropriations rejected it 26–3 on July 31, 2025; outcome unresolved) [LPI, 2025](https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/blog/5-billion-federal-k-12-formula-funding-hangs-balance-between-white-house-and-senate-proposals). ≈$1B in school mental-health grants was cancelled in April 2025 and only ≈$208M re-awarded by December [Education Week, 2025](https://www.edweek.org/policy-politics/a-guide-to-where-school-mental-health-grants-stand-after-a-new-legal-twist/2025/12). The real "why now" is not free money — it is that districts are now self-funding mental health from strained operating budgets (district/school share of school-MH funding rose 58%→65% as the federal share fell 53%→33% [KFF, 2025](https://www.kff.org/mental-health/the-landscape-of-school-based-mental-health-services/)), which raises the bar for anything that isn't tied to a hard outcome like retention.

## 2. What the Public Record Shows

**WISE is real, and substantial — with a correction.** It is the **MedStar Georgetown Center for Wellbeing in School Environments**, a MedStar Health / MedStar Georgetown program in the Department of Psychiatry, delivering multi-tiered school mental health across **≈30 D.C. schools**, reaching ≈14,000 students/families and ≈1,800 staff in SY2023–24 [MedStar WISE 2023 Annual Report](https://www.medstarwise.org/s/2023-MedStar-WISE-Annual-Report.pdf). "≈Decade old" is plausible (a parallel Georgetown/GUCCHD WISE line cites work "since 2013" [TeacherWISE](https://teacherwise.org/)). **Flag:** there is organizational ambiguity between the clinical "MedStar Georgetown WISE" (medstarwise.org) and a "Georgetown WISE / GUCCHD" product line (teacherwise.org). *Which legal entity is spun out, and who owns the IP/brand, must be settled.*

**"Megan" = Dr. Megan Leigh McCormick, PhD** — child clinical psychologist, **Director of Programs and Research** at WISE, Georgetown psychiatry faculty [MedStar WISE](https://www.medstarwise.org/megan). She is **Director, not founder** — if marketed as "founder of WISE," correct it. Other senior figures: Karimah Ware, PsyD (Executive Director); Aaron Rakow, PhD; Matthew Biel, MD [WISE team](https://www.medstarwise.org/team).

**Claims that do not survive the record (confirmed with the team):**
- **"Statewide in Massachusetts"** — there is no live WISE deployment in MA. The statewide programs are separate, non-WISE entities (the BIRCh Project / MA School-Based Behavioral Health TA Center [UMass BIRCh](https://www.umb.edu/birch/resources/); the MA School Mental Health Consortium). The team confirms this is an **RFI pursuit, not a live program** — the report treats MA as pipeline, and the brief's "already statewide" wording should be corrected.
- **"SAMHSA-adjacent credibility halo"** — no direct SAMHSA grant or listing for WISE was found. The nearest SAMHSA tie is *Classroom WISE*, a different "WISE." Treat as **unsubstantiated** unless the team produces a grant number.

**Brand collision is a real risk (team agrees to flag it).** "WISE" is heavily overloaded in education: **Classroom WISE** (SAMHSA/MHTTC-funded educator mental-health training — directly in this space) [classroomwise.org](https://www.classroomwise.org/); the **World Innovation Summit for Education (WISE)**, Qatar Foundation [wise-qatar.org](https://www.wise-qatar.org/); and a UK "Wellbeing in Secondary Education (WISE)" research program. There is also a near-synonym collision on the *concept* — Kaiser Permanente's **RISE ("Resilience in School Environments")** occupies almost the same phrase. A naming/trademark clearance is warranted before committing to "WISE" as a standalone spin-out brand.

**Buyers in motion — mixed.** Education Plus Health (Philadelphia) is a real nonprofit, but its model is school-based *primary care*, and **no public WISE–Education Plus Health connection** exists — confirm the relationship and its scope directly [Education Plus Health](https://educationplushealth.com/). The Utah/Indiana districts and Montana State University funding pursuits have no public footprint (expected for in-progress applications) — treat as founder-asserted pipeline.

### Workarounds & "do nothing"
The true incumbent for *adults* is a cheap EAP plus a one-off PD day. EAPs are near-universal at **≈$1.50–2.00/employee/month with ≈19% utilization** [TASB, 2021](https://www.tasb.org/news-insights/eaps-could-reduce-employee-burnout); one district renewed at **$13.80/employee/year** [Brentwood UFSD, 2024](https://www.bufsd.org/uploaded/BOE/2024-2025/Agendas/7-18-24_Business_Agenda_-Website.pdf). At the low end, **free** offerings own the shelf — Headspace for Educators, Calm Schools, Kaiser Thriving Schools, Pure Edge. "Do nothing" is a strong competitor here precisely because the pain, while real, is chronic rather than acute, and the cheap checkbox (an EAP) lets a district say it has "done something."

### Pricing benchmarks
Pricing across the category is **near-universally quote-based and unpublished** — itself a Stage-3 primary-research gap. The few dated public anchors: CARE retreat $860–990/person (2025); Educators Thriving $500/educator stipend (2023); Yale RULER $6–13k/school + $1k/school/yr; Bright Morning $795–2,995/seat; Panorama district contracts $180k–786k/yr with consultants at $250/hr; on the student-services side for scale reference, Effective School Solutions runs ≈$616–656k/district and Care Solace $47–229k/district. **The most useful internal anchor for the credential layer is Georgetown's own IECMH certificate at ≈$6,000** — a direct comparable for a "WISE-certified consultant" credential and a natural shared-services tie to the Attune/IECMH side. WISE's own pricing model (per-school subscription? per-district? per-credential?) is unset and should be probed against these in discovery.

### White space & positioning
The defensible whitespace is the **integration the others don't do**: a single framework spanning Tier 1 organizational consulting → Tier 2 clinical mental-health consultation with school teams → Tier 3 individual clinical care, anchored by Georgetown research credibility and a trained clinician workforce. CARE owns the evidence but is a single mindfulness intervention; The Teaching Well owns systemic culture work but is contribution-funded; nobody clearly owns "clinically-grounded, research-backed, full-tier educator wellbeing at scale." **A category-wide unit-economics caution:** the broader school-mental-health space is showing post-ESSER strain — Hazel Health's contraction (layoffs, a lost county contract, leadership churn), Second Step's negative net assets, Move This World and CharacterStrong both acquired, and Mindful Schools defunct — evidence that even better-capitalized players struggle to make these economics work once one-time federal money recedes. The 12–18 month threat to monitor is **Panorama-style adjacents** extending their "adult SEL" line into this space with a sales engine WISE can't match, and **free** platforms commoditizing Tier 1. WISE should position in the **PD-consultation + certification price band** ($2.5–4k/day; ≈$90k–650k/district; $3–6k/credential), not as "another school-MH vendor" (crowded, distressed) or "educator-wellbeing content" (cheap/free).

## 5. User / Payor / Referrer Map

### The three roles
- **User (day-to-day):** the classroom teacher and the building principal/leadership team — the principal is the pivotal user, because Tier 1 is organizational and leader-driven, and principals are the ones who feel staff turnover most acutely.
- **Payor (writes the check):** rarely the user. Most plausibly, in sequence: a **funded intermediary/foundation** (first), then a **district** (curriculum/PD or student-services director championing; CFO/superintendent signing under threshold; board ratifying above it), then a **state** agency/legislative line.
- **Referrer / gatekeeper:** the Georgetown/Thrive network and conference relationships (the brief's unsolicited inbound came this way); state associations; and, critically, a foundation program officer who can route WISE into its grantee districts.
- **Same or different?** Different — and the *payor's* job, not the user's, governs the sale.

### Jobs-to-be-Done per role
- **User (principal/teacher):** "help my staff stop burning out and leaving, without adding work I don't have time for." Success = feeling supported (the RAND gap) and lower attrition on their team.
- **Payor (district/state/foundation):** budget, mandate, risk, and reporting. A district CFO's job is to spend constrained operating dollars on something with a defensible ROI (retention dollars saved, or a student-outcome the board cares about) and a clean procurement story. A foundation officer's job is a fundable, evaluable initiative that advances *their* strategy. **This is why "adult wellbeing because it's the right thing" fails to sell and "retention + student outcomes" sells** — the payor's JTBD is not the user's experience.

### Segments & buyer paths (3–5)
| Channel | Who writes the check | Proven funding mechanism | Named buyer in motion? | Read |
|---------|----------------------|--------------------------|------------------------|------|
| Foundation / intermediary pass-through | Health-conversion foundation → intermediary nonprofit → provider | Grant; e.g. Caring for Colorado→CEI $9.5M (≈$500k/district) [Denver Foundation, 2022](https://denverfoundation.org/2022/09/youth-connections-investment-grows-to-9-5-million-to-support-the-social-emotional-mental-well-being-of-colorado-students/); CYBHI→GiveThx $750k/30 schools (1 yr) | Education Plus Health (claimed; WISE link unverified) | **Credible first dollar — but pilot capital, not recurring** |
| Individual districts | District CFO/board (PD, Title II/IV, operating) | Operating budget; 6–18 mo cycle; board-gated | Utah/Indiana (unverified) | Durable if retention-framed; slow; budget-pressured |
| States | Agency or legislative line | Master agreement / RFP; multi-year | MA (RFI pursuit, not live) | Highest ceiling, slowest, most competitive |
| Cooperative purchasing | District via Sourcewell/state MSA | Pre-qualified vendor list | None yet | Compresses repeat sales but multi-month upfront |

### First dollar & sell-to-first
The **foundation/intermediary pass-through is the most realistic first check** (your stated hypothesis is well-supported as a *door-opener*): there are live templates (Caring for Colorado→CEI; Texas Health→Meadows; CYBHI→GiveThx) where staff wellbeing was explicitly in scope and a provider got paid. **But treat it as proof-of-concept capital, not a revenue base** — only ≈12% of foundation grants are multi-year general operating support [CEP, 2020](https://cep.org/report/new-attitudes-old-practices/), and the two biggest funders of this exact space (NoVo, CZI) both exited it. The durable payer is the **district/state operating budget for a contractible service**, which means the sell-to-first sequence is: foundation-seeded pilot → demonstrate retention/outcome → convert the host district to a recurring contract → package onto a Sourcewell/state MSA to make it repeatable. Every segment except the foundation pilot needs Stage-3 primary research (including a paid procurement-database scan, which public web could not substitute for).

## 6. UVP & Unique Advantages

### Pains ↔ pain relievers (Value Proposition Canvas)
| Buyer pain / job | Venture's pain reliever / gain | Fit? |
|------------------|-------------------------------|------|
| District: teacher turnover costs ≈$25k/departure | Tiered program that improves retention; ROI in dollars saved | Strong — *if* retention causation is shown |
| District: needs defensible, board-sellable spend | Georgetown research anchor + outcome framing | Medium — evidence base must be built |
| Principal: staff burning out, no time to add | Done-with-you consultation, not more work for staff | Strong |
| Foundation: fundable, evaluable initiative | Clear tiered model + evaluation partner | Strong (pilot) |
| CFO: cheap checkbox already exists (EAP) | Premium service vs. $14/head EAP | **Weak — price anchor problem** |

### Demonstrable difference vs. the next best alternative
The next best alternative the buyer actually uses is **a cheap EAP + an occasional PD day + free apps** — not a direct competitor. WISE's demonstrable, verifiable difference is the **clinical depth across tiers** (real mental-health consultation and care, not a content library) plus **Georgetown research credibility**. The risk: that difference is verifiable to a clinician but not yet to a CFO, because the *outcome* (retention, student impact) isn't yet measured. "More holistic" is not a differentiator; "X% lower attrition in pilot schools" would be — and producing that is the work.

### Outcomes orientation & unique advantages
Does the market reward the outcome? **Only when the outcome is student- or retention-denominated.** The market does not (yet) price "adult wellbeing" as a terminal good — which is the single most important strategic finding and the reason for the retention/student-outcome framing you chose. Of Thrive's three advantages, **nimble clinical validation is decisive here** — it directly closes the evidence gap that blocks the sale; **learning-partner pilots** are strongly load-bearing (Georgetown-learning-partner framing opens the foundation/district pilot); **field access** helps but is more decorative for this buyer than for Attune's. 

**UVP statement:** _For school and district leaders who are losing teachers they can't afford to replace, WISE provides a clinically-grounded, Georgetown-backed tiered system that improves educator wellbeing and retention — unlike a cheap EAP or a one-off PD day — because it pairs real mental-health consultation with research-designed evidence of impact._

## 7. Thrive Mission Alignment & Studio Fit

**How it scales the mission:** Directly. Thrive's mission is children's mental health *and the workforce that supports it* — educators are explicitly named. WISE serves that workforce and reaches children through it, extending the Center's reach from ≈30 D.C. schools toward a national footprint.
**Where it could go counter to the mission:** Minimal. The one tension is strategic, not values-based — leading the *sale* with student outcomes/retention could, over time, pull the work away from its adult-wellbeing core. Guardrail: keep adult wellbeing as the mechanism even while selling the outcome.
**Fit with the $3–15M sustainable, non-VC posture:** Good on shape (consultation, lean, bootstrappable, no unicorn needed), but reaching the *lower bound* ($3M) is a real question given the WTP headwinds — comparable orgs cluster at $1–4M and lean on contributions.
**Capital / outcome lean:** Spin-out if WTP validates; otherwise a strong **spin-in** (a durable Center program, like Thrive Academy) rather than a forced company. Not an Innovation Hub case.
**Sibling-venture positioning:** Clean neighbor to Attune/Attunify by population (K-12 educators vs. IECMH 0–5), per the team. **But the "consultation" line needs an internal positioning conversation** — WISE Tier 2 (mental-health consultation with school teams) sits adjacent to **Virtual Consultation** (Julia Sayles / Kelli McDermott) and Attune's IECMH consultation; settle the split before customer-facing materials, and exploit shared services (research engine, credentialing, brand).
**Stays at the university vs. spins out:** Research, IP, and the Georgetown/Thrive credibility anchor stay; consultation delivery and GTM go to the entity; Thrive holds equity and is paid in resource cost-recovery. Institutional seam to reconcile: the **MedStar vs. Thrive Center home** of WISE and the medstarwise.org / teacherwise.org entity/IP ambiguity (OTC involvement).

## 8. Operator Priors

No operator is named (co-founder TBD; Megan is the clinical/academic lead, not the operator). The **clinical/IP-lead seat** is filled and strong — Dr. McCormick is a credible, real researcher and the natural framework/IP anchor; she should not be expected to be the CEO. The **operator/CEO seat** needs someone who has sold *contractible services into districts and states* (not a B2C or pure-clinical background), can build category leadership inside a sustainable $3–15M non-VC model, understands K-12 procurement (6–18 month, board-gated cycles), and can stand up the train-the-trainer/credentialing engine that makes the framework scale without dilution. Given the WTP findings, the single highest-value operator trait is **proven district/state revenue motion** — the venture lives or dies on converting pilots to recurring contracts. The named candidates (Nicola, Aubrey, Sam) should be tested specifically against that: *have you personally closed and renewed multi-year district or state service contracts?*

## 9. Answers to the Brief's Open Questions

1. **Willingness to pay — who writes the check?** *(priority)* No dedicated funding for adult wellbeing exists; the credible **first dollar is a foundation/intermediary pass-through** (Education Plus Health-style), but that is pilot capital, not recurring revenue. The **durable check** is a district or state operating-budget contract — winnable only with retention/student-outcome framing. Concern is high but has not converted to a procurement category. *Stage-2/3: confirm the Education Plus Health relationship; scan paid procurement databases; run 3–5 CFO/student-services discovery calls to find the real budget owner.*
2. **Pricing.** Category pricing is opaque/quote-based. Anchors to design against: Yale RULER's $6–13k/school + $1k/school/yr recurring license is the closest structural template; Panorama district contracts ($180k–786k/yr) show the district-scale ceiling; a "WISE-certified consultant" license is plausible. *Stage 3: get 2–3 real quotes from buyers in motion.* The EAP price anchor (≈$14/head) means WISE must price on outcome/ROI, not per-head.
3. **Delivery model.** Virtual consultation is the scalable core; a hybrid with limited in-person convenings plus a **credentialing/train-the-trainer layer** is the leverage that scales reach without dilution (the RULER/TNTP pattern). Pure-virtual maximizes margin; the credential is the durable IP moat.
4. **District-facing vs. clinician capacity-building.** Sequence, don't choose: district/foundation pilots fund and prove the model now; clinician capacity-building is the scaling engine that follows once the framework and evidence are packaged. Leading with clinician-training before there are paying districts risks building supply with no demand.
5. **Relationship to Attune.** Clean neighbors by age band (K-12 vs. 0–5), per the team — but settle the "consultation" positioning internally across WISE / Attune / Virtual Consultation, and share the research/credentialing/brand engine.

## 10. Recommendation & Riskiest Assumptions (Go / No-Go)

**Recommendation: CONDITIONAL GO** — advance to customer discovery, scoped to prove recurring willingness-to-pay. Likely path: spin-out if it validates; spin-in (Center program) if not.

### Signals supporting GO
1. Large, durable, well-quantified problem (highest-burnout U.S. workforce; ≈$25k/departure turnover cost).
2. Real, credible asset — a decade-old Georgetown/MedStar program with a real clinical leader and live delivery in ≈30 schools.
3. Excellent mission fit and a *decisive* Thrive advantage (clinical validation to build the missing evidence base).
4. A real first-dollar template (foundation/intermediary pass-through) and genuine inbound interest.

### Yellow flags / riskiest assumptions (ranked by how fast the venture dies if true)
1. **Willingness-to-pay for adult wellbeing is not real or recurring** (kills it fastest) — no dedicated funding, adverse timing, commoditized/free low end.
2. **The retention/student-outcome causal evidence can't be produced** — without it, the only sellable frame collapses.
3. **The economics top out below $3M ARR** — comparable orgs cluster at $1–4M and lean on contributions; this becomes a spin-in, not a company.
4. **Operator gap** — no proven district/state revenue motion on the team yet.
5. **Brand/claim risk** — "WISE" collision (Classroom WISE) and overstated claims (MA, SAMHSA, evidence base) erode credibility if not corrected.

### Recommended next steps (in execution order)
1. **Confirm the money is real (cheapest, highest-leverage):** verify the Education Plus Health relationship and get one foundation/intermediary to commit a scoped pilot with a retention/outcome metric.
2. Run 3–5 district CFO / student-services discovery calls to locate the real budget owner and pressure-test the retention ROI; scan a paid procurement database (GovSpend/BidNet).
3. Design the evaluation that produces the missing causal evidence (Thrive research engine) — make it a condition of the first pilot.
4. Correct the public claims (MA, SAMHSA, founder/Director) and run a "WISE" naming/trademark clearance.
5. Settle the entity/IP question (MedStar vs. Thrive Center home; medstarwise.org vs. teacherwise.org) and the consultation-positioning split with Virtual Consultation/Attune.

### Open research questions for Stage 3 (customer discovery)
- Will a district pay recurring, non-federal dollars for this, and at what price unit (per-school subscription vs. per-district vs. consultant license)?
- Does educator wellbeing measurably reduce turnover in WISE's own data?
- Can a foundation pilot convert to a district contract within one budget cycle?
- Which single tier (1, 2, or 3) is the wedge buyers will pay for first?
