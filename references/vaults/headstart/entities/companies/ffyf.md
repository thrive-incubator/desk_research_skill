## Current snapshot

FFYF is a bipartisan early-learning funding advocacy organization whose publication stream doubles
as a signal feed for federal child-care policy. It is the clearest public tracker of the two
concurrent 2026 Head Start rulemakings, and in the August 20-27 window it became the most
practically useful one: it published a **searchable section-by-section guide to the rescission
NPRM**, setting each current citation against the proposed change and the remaining statutory
basis, grouped as Governance, Eligibility & Enrollment, Program Requirements, Health & Safety and
Curriculum & Language. The guide documents changes across §§ 1301-1303 touching group sizes,
staff-child ratios, home visitor caseloads, screening timelines and procedural requirements — which
makes it the repositioning worksheet for any vendor whose product is anchored to a named HSPPS
section (see [[hspps-rescission-nprm]]). It also shipped an FAQ on what the proposal would mean for
programs.

Its posture on the rescission remains deliberately reserved — Executive Director Sarah Rittling
said FFYF looks "forward to analyzing it in the coming days and to providing comment on how to
strengthen the program," emphasizing preserving "what makes Head Start, Head Start."

FFYF is also the vault's route into **state-level public opinion**. With Groundwork Ohio it
published an UpONE Insights poll of 600 registered Ohio voters (August 3-6, 2026, ±4%) finding 76%
call finding and affording quality child care a crisis or major problem, 70% support a state- and
federally funded program helping eligible working families afford care (51% of Republicans), and 68%
call federal early-learning funding a good investment — cross-party majorities in a midterm
battleground, alongside its earlier compilation showing 87% national voter support for Head Start.
Its legislative tracking remains the route into the tax-policy channel (see [[seed-act]]) and its
weekly Friday Five is where CCDBG reauthorization mechanics surface — this week via a Bipartisan
Policy Center explainer on the **Child Care Modernization Act**
(see [[hspps-workforce-rule]]). *(as of 2026-W35)*

### 2026-W35 — Polls Ohio with Groundwork Ohio: 76% call child care a crisis, 70% back public funding
*First Five Years Fund / Groundwork Ohio · August 24, 2026* · [link](https://www.ffyf.org/2026/08/24/2026ohiopoll/)
UpONE Insights polled 600 registered Ohio voters August 3-6, 2026 (±4%): 76% say finding and
affording quality child care is a crisis or major problem (62% of Republicans, 78% of Independents,
94% of Democrats); 70% support a state- and federally funded program helping eligible working
families afford child care (51% of Republicans); 68% call federal funding for child care and early
learning a good investment; 60% oppose reducing the number of children served by Ohio's Publicly
Funded Child Care program; half say costs prevent them saving money; 36% are cutting household
essentials; and 19% are putting off starting or expanding a family because of costs.

### 2026-W33 — Covers the SEED Act's Senate passage and lays out the deduction's mechanics
*First Five Years Fund · August 11, 2026* · [link](https://www.ffyf.org/2026/08/11/statement-bipartisan-seed-act-passes-senate/)
FFYF reported that the Supporting Early-Childhood Educators' Deductions (SEED) Act passed the
Senate on August 7, 2026 as part of a broader legislative package, and set out its terms: an
above-the-line deduction of up to $350 annually for classroom supplies, educational materials, and
professional development, against research showing educators spend roughly $860 a year out of
pocket with over 90% reporting such expenses. Executive Director Sarah Rittling called it "another
important victory for early childhood educators, working families, and America's youngest
learners" (also: [FFYF, Aug 7](https://www.ffyf.org/2026/08/07/first-five-things-to-know-about-the-seed-act/)).

### 2026-W32 — Publishes a state-of-play on the two Head Start NPRMs and reserves judgment
*First Five Years Fund · August 6, 2026* · [link](https://www.ffyf.org/2026/08/06/state-of-play-nprm/)
FFYF's tracker treats the August 6 rescission NPRM as the second Head Start NPRM of 2026 alongside
the May 11 workforce proposal, which would remove the 2024 wage-comparability requirements (formal
pay scales, wages comparable to public preschool teachers, implementation deadline August 1, 2031)
and benefits requirements (health insurance, paid leave, behavioral health supports, child care
subsidies, student loan forgiveness, deadline August 1, 2028). Executive Director Sarah Rittling
said FFYF looks "forward to analyzing it in the coming days and to providing comment on how to
strengthen the program" (also: [FFYF, Aug 6](https://www.ffyf.org/2026/08/06/statement-ffyf-executive-director-sarah-rittling-on-proposed-head-start-rule/)).
