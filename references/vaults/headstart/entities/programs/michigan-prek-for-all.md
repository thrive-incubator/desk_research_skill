## Current snapshot

Michigan's Great Start Readiness Program, branded **PreK for All** and reported on by **MiLEAP**,
is the vault's benchmark case for state pre-K expanding while the federal Head Start rulebook
contracts.

As of August 2026 MiLEAP reported **nearly 53,000 applications** on file since June — up **700 in
one month** and **16%** against the same point a year earlier, when applications totaled more than
45,000. Deputy director Emily Laidlaw said **over 55,000 children enrolled for 2025-26**, an
all-time high. Enrollment priority goes to families at or below **400% of the federal poverty
limit** (about **$128,000** for a family of four), though the program is open to all Michigan
residents. The state puts family savings at about **$14,000 a year** per enrolled child.

Entry-strategy read: the eligibility ceiling is the point. At 400% FPL this is a materially larger
addressable population than Head Start's poverty-line eligibility, bought through **state
procurement on state timelines** rather than through ~1,600 individually purchasing federal
grantees. For a founder facing HSPPS uncertainty, state pre-K is the natural hedge — the same
product sold to a different buyer with a different rulebook — and Michigan is the volume case at
16% annual application growth (see [[hspps-rescission-nprm]]). The caveat is that Head Start has no
state pass-through, so a state-pre-K win is a genuinely separate motion rather than a route into the
federal grantee base. *(as of 2026-W35)*
