## Current snapshot

A funder-and-government coalition, launched **June 2024**, that frames child care as economic
development rather than education policy. Its August 2026 report, *Child Care as an Economic
Development Imperative*, draws on work across six places: **Wayne County, Michigan; Pima County,
Arizona; Columbus-Muscogee, Georgia; Multnomah County, Oregon; Cuyahoga County, Ohio; and New
Hampshire statewide.**

The membership is the signal. Partners include the **David and Lucile Packard Foundation**, the
**National Association of Counties**, the **National League of Cities**, and the CDFIs **IFF**,
**Low Income Investment Fund** and **LISC** — community-development finance and county and municipal
government associations, not the education sector. Recommended strategies: employer-funded child
care with matching public and philanthropic support, braiding public and private funding for
facilities, revenue generation for provider sustainability, **second- and third-shift care** for
manufacturing and construction workers, and leveraging employer child care tax credits and
Opportunity Zones. No grant amounts are disclosed.

Entry-strategy read: this is a different buyer with different money — facilities capital, economic
development funds, employer contributions — and **none of it runs through OHS**, which makes it
uncorrelated with HSPPS risk (see [[hspps-rescission-nprm]]). Second- and third-shift care for shift
workers is a genuinely under-built category with a named coalition behind it. Foundation dollars
often seed what federal dollars later scale, so this is the table to watch for what becomes fundable
next; note also that this coalition independently names Columbus-Muscogee, Georgia, which is where
the Blank Family Foundation's IECMH interest is anchored (see [[iecmh-financing-shift]]).
*(as of 2026-W35)*
