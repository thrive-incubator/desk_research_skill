## Current snapshot

The SEED Act extends to early childhood educators the classroom-expense tax deduction K-12 teachers
already have: an **above-the-line deduction of up to $350 annually** for classroom supplies,
educational materials, and professional development, claimable without itemizing. It is bipartisan
and moving — the House passed its version April 27, 2026, the Senate passed it **August 7, 2026**
inside a broader legislative package, and it returns to the House in September 2026 for final
approval before it can go to the President.

The dollar figure is small against the roughly **$860 a year** educators are reported to spend out
of pocket (over 90% report such expenses), but the mechanism is the interesting part: it makes
**professional development a deductible personal expense for the individual educator**, which
quietly opens a direct-to-teacher purchase path in a sector where nearly everything is bought
institutionally. It is also evidence that the bipartisan early-childhood tax channel is still
moving while the same administration proposes to rescind the Performance Standards — the two
channels are not moving together (see [[ffyf]], [[hspps-rescission-nprm]],
[[ece-workforce-compensation]]). *(as of 2026-W33)*

## Timeline

### 2026-W33 — Passes the Senate August 7; returns to the House in September
*First Five Years Fund · August 11, 2026* · [link](https://www.ffyf.org/2026/08/11/statement-bipartisan-seed-act-passes-senate/)
The Supporting Early-Childhood Educators' Deductions (SEED) Act passed the Senate on August 7,
2026 as part of a broader legislative package. Senate sponsors were Susan Collins (R-ME) and
Michael Bennet (D-CO); House sponsors Jimmy Panetta (D-CA), Brian Fitzpatrick (R-PA), Maggie
Goodlander (D-NH), and David Valadao (R-CA). The House passed its version April 27, 2026; the bill
returns to the House in September for final approval. It creates an above-the-line deduction of up
to $350 annually for classroom supplies, educational materials, and professional development,
against research showing educators spend roughly $860 a year out of pocket with over 90% reporting
such expenses (also: [FFYF, Aug 7](https://www.ffyf.org/2026/08/07/first-five-things-to-know-about-the-seed-act/)).
