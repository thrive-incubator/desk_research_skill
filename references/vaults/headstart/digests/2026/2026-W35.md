- **The HSPPS rescission NPRM is now a clock, not a proposal.** "Reducing Federal Burden for Head Start Programs" (RIN 0970-AD30, doc. 2026-16134) published August 7 and **closes for comment on October 6, 2026** — about five and a half weeks from today. It rescinds and replaces the Performance Standards across **all five parts, 45 CFR 1301–1305**. Every product positioning statement built on the 2024 HSPPS is now dated, and the window to shape the replacement is finite and closing.

- **Where the door is opening, it is opening on screening and enrollment, not compliance.** Community-based developmental screening (only 36% of parents nationally complete one) and state pre-K enrollment growth (Michigan at ~53,000 applications) are both expanding while the federal rulebook contracts. That is the shape of the entry opportunity this week: buy-side demand is moving toward reach and capacity metrics, not HSPPS-alignment claims.

## Federal Funding & Policy

### ⚠ HSPPS WATCH: The rescission NPRM's comment period closes October 6, 2026
*Federal Register, Notice of Proposed Rulemaking · RIN 0970-AD30 · doc. 2026-16134 · published August 7, 2026* · [link](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/07/2026-16134/reducing-federal-burden-for-head-start-programs)

"Reducing Federal Burden for Head Start Programs" proposes to **rescind and replace the Head Start Program Performance Standards**, last revised in 2024. Its CFR references cover **45 CFR Parts 1301, 1302, 1303, 1304 and 1305** — the entire HSPPS, not a section of it. ACF's stated aims, in its own words, are to "significantly reduce Federal bureaucratic burden on programs; defer to State policies wherever possible; return substantial local control to Head Start agencies delivering the services and to parents as the primary caregivers and decision-makers for their children; reduce unnecessary duplication of Head Start regulations with Federal statute and other regulations; and emphasize the critical role of health, nutrition, and physical exercise for young children." **Comments close October 6, 2026.**

**So what:** This is the existential item for anyone selling into Head Start. Product positioning that cites a 2024 HSPPS section as the requirement your tool satisfies has a defined shelf life, and the replacement text is still movable until October 6. Two concrete actions: re-anchor claims to the **Head Start Act** (statute, which the NPRM cannot rescind) rather than to regulatory sections, and file a comment — a vendor comment describing what a rescinded requirement would do to an in-flight pilot is on-topic and part of the record.

### Ohio poll: 76% call child care a crisis or major problem; 70% back publicly funded care
*First Five Years Fund / Groundwork Ohio · August 24, 2026* · [link](https://www.ffyf.org/2026/08/24/2026ohiopoll/)

UpONE Insights polled **600 registered Ohio voters, August 3–6, 2026, ±4%**. Findings: **76%** say the ability to find and afford quality child care is a crisis or major problem (62% of Republicans, 78% of Independents, 94% of Democrats); **70%** support a state- and federally funded program helping eligible working families afford child care (51% of Republicans); **68%** say federal funding for child care and early learning for low-income working families is a good investment; **60%** oppose reducing the number of children served by Ohio's Publicly Funded Child Care program. The poll contains no Head Start-specific results.

**So what:** Cross-party majorities for publicly funded care in a midterm battleground is the political counterweight to the deregulation push, and it is usable evidence in a pitch deck aimed at state agencies or advocacy-adjacent funders. The 19% family-formation number is the sharpest single statistic in the week for an economic-argument slide.

### Michigan pre-K applications reach nearly 53,000, up 16% year over year
*The 74 · August 23, 2026* · [link](https://www.the74million.org/zero2eight/nearly-53000-apply-for-michigans-free-pre-k-program-as-enrollment-grows/)

MiLEAP reported **nearly 53,000 applications** on file since June for the Great Start Readiness Program / PreK for All — up **700 in one month** and **16%** against the same point last year, when applications totaled more than 45,000. **So what:** State pre-K expanding at 16% a year while the federal rulebook contracts is the clearest structural signal in this digest. A 400%-FPL eligibility ceiling puts Michigan's program in a different market from Head Start's poverty-line eligibility — far larger addressable population, state procurement rather than federal grantee purchasing. For a founder facing HSPPS uncertainty, state pre-K is the natural hedge, and Michigan is the volume case.

### The early childhood policy leadership pipeline is thin, and a Columbia-housed initiative is trying to widen it
*Hechinger Report · August 24, 2026* · [link](https://hechingerreport.org/opinion-the-leaders-who-are-shaping-early-childhood-education-need-better-more-professional-preparation/)

Michael H. Levine, writing for **ECPIHE** (Early Childhood Policy in Institutions of Higher Education, housed at Teachers College, Columbia University), argues the field lacks trained leaders able to translate policy into working systems: "early childhood policy decisions are made every day by people who care deeply about children but were prepared to do something else entirely." ### Irish hospital study points at the IECMH implementation gap: staffing, shared language, reflective supervision
*WAIMH Perspectives · August 21, 2026* · [link](https://perspectives.waimh.org/2026/08/21/keeping-the-baby-in-mind-staff-and-parent-perspectives-on-infant-care-in-paediatric-hospital-settings/)

A mixed-methods cross-sectional survey across four sites of Children's Health Ireland, with **13 parents** of infants hospitalized two weeks or longer and **56 staff**, collected May–September 2023. Parents emphasized bonding opportunities and valued staff encouragement of skin-to-skin contact and holding. Staff were committed to developmental care but reported insufficient time from staffing shortages and high clinical acuity, and **variability in how staff understood developmental care**, suggesting no shared organizational language. Recommendations: formal infant mental health networks with designated champions, ongoing education, "zero separation" policies, better spaces for family togetherness, structured **reflective supervision**, and training on the **Newborn Behavioral Observation** system.

**So what:** Irish setting, so treat the specifics as read-across rather than US evidence. But the failure mode it names is the one every IECMH initiative hits in US settings too, Early Head Start included: the framework is adopted, the shared language and the supervision time are not. That is an implementation-support product thesis — reflective supervision infrastructure and staff-facing shared-language tooling — and it is more defensible than another curriculum.

### A Spanish-language version of the PICCOLO caregiver-interaction measure is validated for children with disabilities
*Topics in Early Childhood Special Education · August 24, 2026* · [link](https://doi.org/10.1177/02711214261478139)

The abstract reports that PICCOLO is an observational tool assessing parent–child interactions that gives practitioners an instrument to guide practice, but that it **had not been validated in children with developmental disorders such as autism and/or intellectual disability** — a population among those most likely to need it. **So what:** Measures become procurement requirements.
