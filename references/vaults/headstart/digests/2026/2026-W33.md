# Thrive Head Start Synthesis
### Week of 2026-08-06 – 2026-08-13

## This Week's Throughline

- **A new Information Memorandum is the operational half of the deregulation story, and almost nobody is covering it.** ACF-OHS-IM-26-01, *Enrollment Reductions*, issued August 6, rewrites how OHS reviews Change in Scope requests to cut funded enrollment. Its framing is blunt: Change in Scope requests jumped from **39 in FY2021 to 746 in FY2024** — an increase of over 1,800% — and an ASPE analysis cited in the IM attributes **more than 97,000 eliminated slots** to that period. OHS now offers a **30-day expedited pathway** only when a grantee gives back federal dollars proportionally; everything else goes to comprehensive review requiring waiting-list counts, per-slot funding benchmarked against ASPE state medians, and support letters from the Governor, Members of Congress, the Head Start Collaboration Office director, and the **state CCDF administrator**.
- Read together with last week's rescission NPRM, the direction is coherent: **strip the compliance rulebook, defend the slot count.** The administration is deregulating what programs must *do* while making it materially harder for them to serve *fewer children*. For a vendor, that flips the pitch — capacity, enrollment, and waiting-list conversion are now the metrics OHS itself scores, while the HSPPS-alignment claim keeps depreciating.
- **The rescission NPRM formally published August 7** (docket ACF-2026-0595), starting the 60-day comment clock, and the week was consumed by provider and state reaction — Washington Post, NPR, Politico, PBS, Education Week, and a dense layer of state coverage from Wisconsin, Pennsylvania, Minnesota, New Jersey, New York, and Texas.
- **A genuinely new market opened**: OHS announced intent to award **$7.2M in sole-source grants — $3.6M each to the Federated States of Micronesia and the Republic of the Marshall Islands** — to stand up Early Head Start and Head Start Preschool in the Freely Associated States under the Compacts of Free Association Amendments Act of 2024. Two brand-new grantees, 60-month periods, building from zero.
- **The awards channel went fully silent**: zero 93.600 transactions posted in the window, a third consecutive quiet week. Treat as reporting lag plus the pre-October lull, not a funding stop — but it means USASpending gives no independent read on how grantees are responding.
- On the workforce side, the most interesting item wasn't a policy at all: early childhood programs are **buying housing for their teachers** because they cannot raise wages — a Connecticut model now replicating in Missouri, Michigan, and Georgia.

## Federal Funding & Policy

### ⚠ HSPPS WATCH: OHS issues IM-26-01, tightening review of enrollment-reduction requests
*Information Memorandum · ACF-OHS-IM-26-01 · issued August 6, 2026* · [link](https://headstart.gov/policy/im/acf-ohs-im-26-01)

Addressed to **all non-Tribal Head Start grant recipients**, the IM establishes a review process for Change in Scope requests that reduce funded enrollment — an authority grantees hold under **Section 640(g)(3)** of the Head Start Act to reduce enrollment in order to maintain quality services. The stated cause is volume: OHS says the surge in requests has "contributed to prolonged review times."

The background section is the most quotable federal statement yet on the last administration's record:

- **746 requests for enrollment reductions in FY2024 versus 39 in FY2021** — "an increase of over 1,800 percent."
- An **ASPE report** (Schreier & Benton, 2026) cited in the IM finds actions between FY2022 and FY2024 "resulted in the elimination of more than 97,000 slots."
- The 2024 final rule *Supporting the Head Start Workforce and Consistent Quality Programming* (89 FR 67720) is blamed for placing **more than 100,000 slots at risk nationally — approximately 84,000 HSP and 22,000 EHS**.

The mechanics:

- **Two pathways.** A reduction in funded enrollment **paired with a proportional reduction in federal funding** gets expedited consideration with "an intended timeline of no more than 30 days." A reduction **without** a proportional funding cut goes to comprehensive review.
- **All requests**: submit at least **90 days before planned implementation**, via HSES as an Amendment: change in scope, with SF-424, SF-424A, governing board and Policy Council approvals, program and budget narratives, and a proposed program schedule.
- **Comprehensive review adds a cover sheet** with: current vs. proposed funded slots and percentage change; current vs. proposed budget and percentage change; **current vs. proposed per-slot federal funding compared to ASPE-reported state-specific medians**; estimated children in the community in the age range and needing care; estimated Head Start–eligible children; **the number of children on the program's waiting list**; and an attestation that the change will not create barriers for parents or children.
- **Community support must be documented in writing** from stakeholders that may include the Governor, Members of Congress, state/Tribal/local elected officials, the **Head Start Collaboration Office director**, the **state or Tribal CCDF administrator**, community organizations, and affected parents — each letter stating support or opposition and acknowledging the percent change in slots, budget, and per-slot spending.
- OHS weighs completeness, demonstrated community need, effect on eligible families, **availability of alternative child care**, financial and operational viability, community support, and compliance history. "An expedited review does not guarantee approval." Signed by **Lizbeth Casco White, Director, Office of Head Start**.

**So what:** This is the operational counterweight to the rescission NPRM, and it changes the buyer's problem. A grantee that wants to shrink now has to produce a defensible community-needs estimate, a waiting-list number, a per-slot cost benchmarked to a state median, and a stakeholder-letter campaign — or hand back money. Three openings follow. (1) **Community needs assessment and enrollment-demand analytics** just became a graded federal deliverable with a named data structure — the IM effectively publishes the schema. (2) **Waiting-list and ERSEA conversion tooling** is no longer a nice-to-have; the waiting-list count is now evidence in a federal determination. (3) **Per-slot cost accounting** — grantees have to compare their own per-slot federal funding to an ASPE state median, which is exactly the kind of number most programs cannot produce on demand from their current fiscal stack. Note the audience limit: non-Tribal grantees only, and the IM explicitly does not cover HSP-to-EHS slot conversions.

### OHS opens two new grantees in the Pacific: $7.2M sole-source to Micronesia and the Marshall Islands
*Federal Register · Notice of intent to award sole-source awards · August 12, 2026* · [link](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/12/2026-16392/announcement-of-the-intent-to-award-sole-source-awards-to-the-federated-states-of-micronesia-and-the)

ACF/OHS announced intent to award **two sole-source grants totaling up to $7,200,000 — $3,600,000 each** — to the **Federated States of Micronesia** and the **Republic of the Marshall Islands** to support the establishment and provision of Early Head Start and/or Head Start Preschool services. The awards run on new statutory authority: the **Compacts of Free Association Amendments Act of 2024** (Division G, Title II of Public Law 118-42) plus FY2026 Labor-HHS appropriations providing **$8 million** to extend Head Start eligibility and services in the Freely Associated States. **So what:** Two greenfield grantees standing up Head Start from nothing on five-year money — no legacy vendor stack, no incumbent SIS, no entrenched curriculum. That is the rarest thing in this market. It is also small, remote, and operationally hard, and the sole-source structure means the entity, not a competition, decides procurement. Worth a note in a pipeline, not a pivot.

### The SEED Act passes the Senate: a $350 above-the-line deduction for early childhood educators
*First Five Years Fund · August 11, 2026* · [link](https://www.ffyf.org/2026/08/11/statement-bipartisan-seed-act-passes-senate/)

The **Supporting Early-Childhood Educators' Deductions (SEED) Act** passed the Senate on **August 7** as part of a broader legislative package. It extends to early childhood educators the classroom-expense deduction K-12 teachers already have: an **above-the-line deduction of up to $350 annually** for classroom supplies, educational materials, and professional development, claimable without itemizing. FFYF notes research showing educators spend roughly **$860 a year** out of pocket, with **over 90%** reporting such expenses. Senate sponsors were Susan Collins (R-ME) and Michael Bennet (D-CO); House sponsors Jimmy Panetta (D-CA), Brian Fitzpatrick (R-PA), Maggie Goodlander (D-NH), and David Valadao (R-CA). The House passed its version April 27; the bill returns to the House in September for final approval (also: [First Five Years Fund, Aug 7](https://www.ffyf.org/2026/08/07/first-five-things-to-know-about-the-seed-act/)).

**So what:** $350 is not a market, but the mechanism is worth watching: it makes **professional development a deductible personal expense for the individual educator**, which quietly creates a direct-to-teacher purchase path in a sector where almost everything is bought institutionally. Bipartisan movement on early childhood tax policy also survives in a week when the same administration is proposing to rescind the Performance Standards — the two channels are not moving together.

### HRSA sends performance measures for its pediatric–early childhood integration program to OMB
*Federal Register · Notice · August 10, 2026* · **Comments close September 9, 2026** · [link](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/10/2026-16227/agency-information-collection-activities-submission-to-omb-for-review-and-approval-public-comment)

HRSA submitted an information collection request for **Transforming Pediatrics for Early Childhood (TPEC) Program Performance Measures**, authorized under Title V, Sec. 501(a)(2) of the Social Security Act. TPEC "directly funds organizations with statewide or tribal reach to place early childhood development experts in local pediatric practices to deliver team-based care to young children and their families." All recipients will collect and report standardized annual performance measures so they can "access, analyze, and use pediatric practice-level and state-level data." Burden estimate: **10 respondents, 1 response each, 120 hours per response, 1,200 total annual burden hours**. The prior 60-day notice (April 16, 2026) drew no public comments.

**So what:** TPEC is a small program — 10 recipients — but it is one of the few federal vehicles putting **early childhood developmental expertise inside pediatric primary care**, which is where the IECMH field has been arguing the leverage sits. The measure set now going to OMB is the definition of success those grantees will report against; getting a product's outputs to map to it is cheaper before the measures are locked than after.

### Programs are buying housing for teachers because they cannot raise wages
*The 74 · August 13, 2026* · [link](https://www.the74million.org/zero2eight/more-early-childhood-programs-are-providing-free-housing-to-teaching-staff/)

Friends Center for Children, a nonprofit early care and education program with four locations in New Haven, houses **10 of its 47 teachers for free** and has two more moving in this fall, targeting **30% of its teaching staff by 2028**. The initiative began in 2020 after CEO Allyx Schiavone concluded the organization could not budget salary increases without more public funding or higher tuition, and instead went after teachers' largest expense. The economics: Connecticut early childhood educators earn about **$31,000 a year** (Early Childhood Workforce Index), against a state median rent of about **$24,000 a year** (Zillow). Housing stock comes from donated properties plus a partnership with the **Yale School of Architecture**, whose graduate students design and build roughly one home a year — homes worth about **$750,000** that Friends Center acquires for about **$300,000**. Five teachers now live as neighbors in an area they call "the Village," a five-minute walk from the program. Every housed teacher meets **monthly with a financial coach**. More than **1,000 visitors** have come to New Haven since 2020, and the model is now replicating in **Missouri, Michigan, and Georgia** — Urban Sprouts in St. Louis (about 130 children, birth to five, one location) launched a two-teacher pilot this year. Separately, a new Harvard analysis argued the answer to the child care crisis is simply to pay workers more (also: [The Boston Globe, Aug 12](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiiwFBVV95cUxNMzNjcTdveFRxd05kSWRvWlpMdkFEOHlZTldjaGJZV2tDZmtJa01UdGJvNkNTSmRiSGZzWTRrU2N4X3pWRHdMSzBtdGdpOVNMWDJIdlprTmNpV1djUVNFS3JwUGJ4WU5LNmNaYlJhM3ljQ2ZMM0RiN3Rqd1R5WkkySmpKMDNkV1pZWDZB?oc=5)).

**So what:** This is the workforce crisis expressed as a balance-sheet workaround, and it says something useful about where grantee money can actually go. Compensation is capped by the funding formula; **benefits, logistics, and financial-wellbeing supports are not** — and under the NPRM's proposed 5% administrative cost cap, a benefit that reads as a program cost is structurally advantaged over one that reads as overhead. Products that reduce a teacher's cost of living or cost of working — financial coaching, benefits navigation, transportation, credentialing pathways — are selling into the one lever a program director still controls. Note also the shape of the diffusion: 1,000 site visits and a peer-to-peer replication chain, with no vendor anywhere in it.

## Technology & Vendor Layer

### A perinatal mental health RCT finds the self-guided digital program worked — and phone coaching added nothing
*WAIMH Perspectives · August 7, 2026* · [link](https://perspectives.waimh.org/2026/08/07/blending-digital-prevention-and-clinical-care-the-parents-and-babies-program-in-perinatal-and-mental-health/)

The **Parents and Babies Program** ("Toi, Moi, Bébé") is a free, bilingual French/English online program for pregnant people, built from brief didactic content, videos, audio, interactive exercises, and tip sheets in roughly 20-minute modules that can be taken in any order, self-selected or clinician-recommended. A randomized controlled trial run **2021–2024 with 510 pregnant women** found the program, **with or without telephone support, was superior to usual care** in reducing depression postnatally — and that **telephone coaching added no additional benefit beyond the self-help version**. It has been deployed across Quebec through CHU Sainte-Justine. The authors are explicit that digital tools extend clinical relationships rather than replace them: online programs are "a little help that goes a long way."

**So what:** The null result on telephone coaching is the commercially significant finding, and it cuts directly against the prevailing "digital plus human coach" model that most IECMH platforms price around. If a self-guided module set matches a coached one on outcomes at a fraction of the marginal cost, the unit economics of perinatal mental health products change — and the case for selling into a program at population scale, rather than per-caseload, gets stronger. Caveats before anyone builds a deck on it: Quebec sample, publicly funded health system, one trial. But this is the second consecutive week WAIMH has published an operating thesis for digital IECMH tools, and both land on the same line — augment the relationship, don't staff it.

### Outcomes-based contracting keeps spreading — Michigan now requires it for $50M in virtual tutoring
*Hechinger Report · August 10, 2026* · [link](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-outcomes-basedcontracting/)

A new WestEd evaluation of outcomes-based contracting — where part of a vendor's payment depends on whether students actually use the service and hit agreed academic goals — looked at eight districts across California, Florida, Mississippi, and Texas from August 2024 to March 2026. Of **10 interventions**, only **4** could be rigorously assessed; **3 showed measurable academic gains**. AI-powered reading software significantly improved second-grade proficiency but showed no benefit for older elementary students. There was **no evidence of cost savings** — overall spending resembled traditional contracts. Michigan now requires the approach to access **$50 million** for virtual tutoring, and 20-plus states have adopted some version. The Center for Outcomes Based Contracting spent roughly **$80,000 per district** in coaching and technical assistance and is testing models under $10,000. In the same week, a major virtual tutoring provider shut down with experts citing a lack of evidence (also: [The 74, Aug 10](https://www.the74million.org/article/major-virtual-tutoring-provider-shuts-down-experts-cite-lack-of-evidence/)).

**So what:** K-12 for now, but this is the purchasing model that the Head Start world is being pushed toward from the other direction. When the HSPPS stops naming the instrument and the mandate becomes a local budget decision, the surviving basis for a sale is demonstrated effect — and outcomes-based contracting is the procurement machinery for exactly that. Two things to take from the evaluation: the youngest-grade result was the strongest one, and **only 4 of 10 interventions could even be evaluated rigorously**, which means measurability is becoming a product requirement rather than an evaluation afterthought. Build the outcome instrumentation in, or be unpurchasable under the model that is arriving.

## Research

### Coaching-based training in a US early-intervention model held its fidelity with displaced professionals in a refugee context
*Infants & Young Children · August 12, 2026* · [link](https://doi.org/10.1097/iyc.0000000000000342)

The abstract reports a study of **20 displaced Ukrainian early intervention professionals in Poland** trained and simultaneously coached to deliver **Family Guided Routines Based Intervention (FGRBI)** to refugee families with young children at risk for or experiencing developmental delays. The study assessed change in implementation fidelity over time and compared self-assessed against trainer-rated scores.

**So what:** The sample is not US, but the model is — FGRBI is a US-developed, routines-based Part C approach, and what is being tested here is the **training-plus-simultaneous-coaching delivery model** under maximally adverse conditions. That is the same mechanism most early-intervention and home-visiting product companies rely on when they claim a workforce can be brought to fidelity quickly. The self-rated vs. trainer-rated comparison is the practical part: if self-assessment tracks trainer ratings, remote and self-administered fidelity monitoring gets cheaper, which is the difference between a coaching product that scales and one that needs an observer in every session.

### Teacher-child conversation quality varies by classroom context — and summer is understudied
*Early Childhood Education Journal · August 10, 2026* · [link](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10643-026-02313-6)

The abstract reports a study of **six teachers across three preschool centers in a Northeastern US state**, serving children ages 3–5 from low- and middle-income backgrounds, examining how frequently teachers used linguistic strategies designed to promote rich teacher-child conversation. Teacher-child talk was video-recorded across **three classroom contexts — mealtimes, free play, and group time** — during the summer months.

**So what:** Small and descriptive, but it sits on a live commercial question. With the NPRM proposing to drop **CLASS: Pre-K as the named DRS instrument**, the field is about to have an argument about what counts as evidence of interaction quality and who measures it. Studies that show quality varies systematically by context — and that mealtimes and free play carry measurable instructional value — support a shift toward continuous, naturalistic observation over a scheduled annual visit. That is a product category, and it is currently under-occupied in early childhood.
