## Current snapshot

The federal government has decided that Head Start's slot count is the number it will defend, and
**ACF-OHS-IM-26-01, *Enrollment Reductions* (issued August 6, 2026)** is the instrument. The Head
Start Act has always let programs propose a reduction of funded enrollment to maintain quality
services (**Section 640(g)(3)**); what changed is the review standard applied to those Change in
Scope requests, and the volume that provoked it.

The numbers OHS puts on the record: Change in Scope requests for enrollment reductions rose from
**39 in FY2021 to 746 in FY2024**, "an increase of over 1,800 percent." An **ASPE report** (Schreier
& Benton, 2026) cited in the IM finds FY2022-FY2024 actions "resulted in the elimination of more
than 97,000 slots." OHS attributes the surge in part to the 2024 final rule *Supporting the Head
Start Workforce and Consistent Quality Programming* (89 FR 67720), which it says was estimated to
put **more than 100,000 slots at risk — approximately 84,000 HSP and 22,000 EHS**
(see [[hspps-workforce-rule]]).

**The mechanism.** Two pathways. A reduction in funded enrollment **paired with a proportional
reduction in federal funding** gets expedited consideration, "an intended timeline of no more than
30 days." A reduction **without** a proportional funding cut goes to comprehensive review. All
requests go through HSES as an Amendment: change in scope at least **90 days** before planned
implementation, carrying SF-424, SF-424A, governing board and Policy Council approvals, program and
budget narratives, and a proposed schedule. Comprehensive review adds a cover sheet with current vs.
proposed funded slots, budget, and **per-slot federal funding compared to ASPE-reported
state-specific medians**; estimated children in the community in the age range and needing care;
estimated Head Start-eligible children; **the program's waiting-list count**; and an attestation
that the change will not create barriers for parents or children. It also requires **written
evidence of community support or opposition** from stakeholders that may include the Governor,
Members of Congress, state/Tribal/local elected officials, the **Head Start Collaboration Office
director**, the **state or Tribal CCDF administrator**, community organizations and affected
parents — each letter acknowledging the percent change in slots, budget and per-slot spending. OHS
weighs completeness, community need, effect on families, availability of alternative child care,
financial and operational viability, community support and compliance history; "an expedited review
does not guarantee approval." Scope limits: **non-Tribal grant recipients only**, and it explicitly
does not address HSP-to-EHS slot conversions.

**Entry-strategy read.** This is the operational counterweight to the HSPPS rescission — strip the
compliance rulebook, defend the slot count (see [[hspps-rescission-nprm]]) — and it converts three
soft capabilities into graded federal deliverables. (1) **Community needs assessment and
enrollment-demand analytics**: the IM effectively publishes the schema OHS will score. (2)
**Waiting-list and ERSEA conversion tooling**: the waiting-list count is now evidence in a federal
determination, not an internal metric. (3) **Per-slot cost accounting**: grantees must compare their
own per-slot federal funding to an ASPE state median, a number most programs cannot produce on
demand from their current fiscal stack. The stakeholder-letter requirement also makes the state
collaboration office director and the state CCDF administrator procurement-adjacent relationships.
*(as of 2026-W33)*

## Timeline

### 2026-W33 — OHS issues IM-26-01 establishing two review pathways for enrollment reductions
*Information Memorandum · ACF-OHS-IM-26-01 · issued August 6, 2026* · [link](https://headstart.gov/policy/im/acf-ohs-im-26-01)
Addressed to all non-Tribal Head Start grant recipients, IM-26-01 sets standards for Change in
Scope requests reducing funded enrollment under Section 640(g)(3) of the Head Start Act. Expedited
review with "an intended timeline of no more than 30 days" applies when the reduction is paired
with a proportional reduction in federal funding; otherwise comprehensive review. All requests:
HSES Amendment at least 90 days before implementation with SF-424, SF-424A, governing board and
Policy Council approvals, program and budget narratives. Comprehensive reviews add a cover sheet
(current vs. proposed slots, budget, and per-slot federal funding vs. ASPE-reported state medians;
community child counts; waiting-list count; a no-barriers attestation) and written stakeholder
support or opposition from parties including the Governor, Members of Congress, the Head Start
Collaboration Office director and the state or Tribal CCDF administrator. Background figures: 746
enrollment-reduction requests in FY2024 vs. 39 in FY2021 (over 1,800 percent increase); an ASPE
report finding more than 97,000 slots eliminated FY2022-FY2024; the 2024 workforce final rule (89 FR
67720) estimated to place more than 100,000 slots at risk (approximately 84,000 HSP, 22,000 EHS).
Signed by Lizbeth Casco White, Director, Office of Head Start.
