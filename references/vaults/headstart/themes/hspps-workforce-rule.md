## Current snapshot

The Head Start workforce-requirements rulemaking ("Restoring Flexibility to Support Head Start
Program Access," NPRM published May 11-12, 2026; comments closed June 11, 2026) is still pending —
**the final rule has not landed on the ACF docket as of 2026-W33**, more than two months after
comments closed. FFYF's tracker sets out what it would remove: the 2024 **wage** requirements
(formal pay scales for all staff, wages comparable to public preschool teachers, salaries covering
basic living costs, wage comparability between Head Start Preschool and Early Head Start staff —
implementation deadline **August 1, 2031**) and the 2024 **benefits** requirements (health
insurance and paid leave, behavioral health supports, access to child care subsidies and student
loan forgiveness, periodic reassessment — deadline **August 1, 2028**).

As of this week it is no longer the only live Head Start rulemaking. On August 6, 2026 OHS filed a
far larger NPRM proposing to rescind and replace the entire HSPPS
(see [[hspps-rescission-nprm]]) — so the workforce final rule could now publish *while* the
wholesale rescission is still in comment, and the two would interact. The docket's deregulatory
cadence has become explicit in its own rule titles ("Reducing Bureaucracy and Burden…", July 31
and August 3), which raises the odds both are finalized in some form (see [[acf]]). Watch both:
an HSPPS change can invalidate a portfolio company's positioning mid-pilot, and the wage/benefits
removals in particular interact with the Brookings finding that child care pay has barely moved
relative to the labor market in 35 years (see [[ece-workforce-compensation]]).

**OHS has now put the government's rationale on the record.** IM-26-01 (August 6, 2026) states
that the 2024 final rule *Supporting the Head Start Workforce and Consistent Quality Programming*
(89 FR 67720) "imposed mandatory compensation-related requirements" estimated to place **more than
100,000 Head Start slots at risk nationally — approximately 84,000 HSP and 22,000 EHS**, and links
it to a surge in enrollment-reduction requests (39 in FY2021 to 746 in FY2024) and an ASPE finding
of **more than 97,000 slots eliminated** between FY2022 and FY2024. That is the clearest statement
yet of the theory of the case behind the pending final rule: the wage and benefits mandates are
being framed as the cause of the slot loss the same administration is now moving to stop
(see [[enrollment-reduction-review]]). Whatever one makes of the causal claim, it makes the final
rule more likely to land in the direction of removal. *(as of 2026-W33)*

## Timeline

### 2026-W33 — OHS blames the 2024 workforce rule for putting 100,000+ slots at risk
*Information Memorandum · ACF-OHS-IM-26-01 · issued August 6, 2026* · [link](https://headstart.gov/policy/im/acf-ohs-im-26-01)
IM-26-01's background section states that the 2024 final rule *Supporting the Head Start Workforce
and Consistent Quality Programming* (89 FR 67720) "imposed mandatory compensation-related
requirements" and "was estimated to place more than 100,000 Head Start slots at risk nationally,
including approximately 84,000 HSP slots and 22,000 EHS slots." It pairs that with a rise in Change
in Scope requests from 39 in FY2021 to 746 in FY2024 and an ASPE finding that FY2022-FY2024 actions
eliminated more than 97,000 slots. The workforce final rule remains absent from the ACF docket.

### 2026-W32 — Final rule still absent as a second, larger Head Start NPRM lands
*First Five Years Fund · August 6, 2026* · [link](https://www.ffyf.org/2026/08/06/state-of-play-nprm/)
FFYF's state-of-play sets out the workforce NPRM's contents — removal of the 2024 wage-comparability
requirements (formal pay scales, wages comparable to public preschool teachers, deadline August 1,
2031) and benefits requirements (health insurance, paid leave, behavioral health supports, child
care subsidies, student loan forgiveness, deadline August 1, 2028) — and treats it as the first of
two 2026 Head Start NPRMs, the second being the August 6 rescission proposal. The workforce final
rule has still not appeared on the ACF docket; comments closed June 11.

### 2026-W32 — Brookings quantifies the problem the wage requirements addressed
*Brookings · August 4, 2026* · [link](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMimgFBVV95cUxQLUdTaERVR1RnTEpybFJDOEdDQlNUbVhMZ2UxZnZjeGxJcmpWZHFGZEFpdkNfRHpmajRabmdGUVlvRE52SDdub29CVXVtSmFWNl91YVdWVF9TUnZpWFdua2VPcFkybmVodkRrTjZmVDI1SUJ6S3c0M1JvSFNGM3BqSVlhM0RlejlENC1UNF9zWVlFdjhoZWxDT053?oc=5)
Average hourly wages for child care workers reached $22.15 in 2025 dollars, up from $14.25 in the
early 1990s, but the gap against all women widened from about $20,000 annually in 1990-1995 to
$28,000 by 2020-2025, and child care workers still earn roughly $8,000 less annually than other
low-wage workers despite being better educated (66% with some college vs. 52%).

### 2026-W29 — No HSPPS movement; ACF docket shows deregulatory cadence
*Federal Register · Rule · July 14, 2026* · [link](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/14/2026-14131/designated-placement-requirements-under-titles-iv-e-and-iv-b-for-lgbtqi-children-rescission)
No new IMs or PIs posted, and the workforce final rule has not yet appeared on the ACF docket
(comments closed June 11). The week's sourced docket action was a final rule rescinding the
designated-placement requirements under Titles IV-E and IV-B — child-welfare regulation, not
HSPPS, but a data point on the deregulatory cadence of the docket the workforce rule will land on.
