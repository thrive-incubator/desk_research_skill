## Current snapshot

Developmental screening is being relocated out of the pediatric office at the same moment the
federal requirement to do it inside Head Start is proposed for rescission. Those two movements are
what make this an arc rather than a product category.

**The incumbent channel is failing measurably.** Only about **36% of parents nationally** report
completing a developmental screener in the previous 12 months; in **New York the rate is 28%**. The
response is explicitly to move screening to non-clinical settings: **Help Me Grow**, a growing
network of local organizations, is running screenings in libraries, farmers markets and zoos, and New
York's Council on Children and Families has announced a campaign to close the gap, with the
Adirondack Birth to Three Alliance participating.

**The federal mandate is going the other way.** The August 6, 2026 HSPPS rescission NPRM would
rescind **§ 1302.33 child screenings and assessments** and drop CLASS: Pre-K as the named DRS
instrument, leaving the Head Start Act's statutory screening duties intact but removing the
prescriptive federal implementation — cadences, timelines, named instruments
(see [[hspps-rescission-nprm]]). FFYF's section-by-section guide confirms **screening timelines** as
one of the parameters in play. So the buyer stops being a compliance officer executing a required
cadence and becomes a program director choosing whether and how to screen.

**The literature is arriving on the same question.** Two peer-reviewed papers registered in the
August 20-27 window sit directly on this arc. A **Play and Participation Screening Service (PPSS)**
was developed and piloted inside early education and care centres to detect children needing
additional support, evaluated through a mixed-methods feasibility study against five criteria —
acceptability, practicality, implementation, preliminary efficacy and demand. And the **PICCOLO**
observational measure of parent-child interaction was validated in a Spanish-language version for
children with developmental disorders such as autism and/or intellectual disability, a population it
had not previously been validated in. Both are non-US or unspecified settings, so treat specifics as
read-across.

Entry-strategy read: a 36% completion rate is a quantified failure of the clinical channel, and
Head Start and Early Head Start are already in the non-clinical settings the field is moving toward.
A screening tool built for a **family advocate or a librarian rather than a pediatrician**, with
results that route into referral, is a defensible category — and with § 1302.33 gone, it has to be
sold on outcomes and demand rather than on compliance. Note the evaluation frame the PPSS study
used: acceptability, practicality and demand come *before* efficacy, which is the order a skeptical
grantee will apply too. On measures, Spanish-language validation plus disability-inclusive
validation is precisely the pair of gaps that disqualifies a tool in a Head Start review, where
dual-language learners and IDEA-eligible children are both large populations. *(as of 2026-W35)*

## Timeline

### 2026-W35 — A Spanish-language PICCOLO is validated for children with disabilities
*Topics in Early Childhood Special Education · August 24, 2026* · [link](https://doi.org/10.1177/02711214261478139)
The abstract reports that PICCOLO is an observational tool assessing parent-child interactions that
gives practitioners an instrument to guide practice, but that it had not been validated in children
with developmental disorders such as autism and/or intellectual disability — among the populations
most likely to need it. This paper validates the Spanish version for assessing caregiver strengths
in supporting development in children with disabilities. The deposited abstract is truncated before
the results, so no effect sizes or sample are recorded.

### 2026-W35 — A play-and-participation screening service is piloted inside early education settings
*Early Childhood Education Journal · August 21, 2026* · [link](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10643-026-02335-0)
The abstract reports that a Play and Participation Screening Service (PPSS) was developed and
piloted in early education and care centres to detect children needing additional support,
evaluated through a mixed-methods feasibility study using action research against five criteria:
acceptability, practicality, implementation, preliminary efficacy and demand. The terminology
indicates a non-US setting.

### 2026-W35 — Only 36% of parents complete a developmental screener; Help Me Grow moves screening to libraries and farmers markets
*Hechinger Report · August 20, 2026* · [link](https://hechingerreport.org/developmental-screening-outside-the-doctors-office/)
Help Me Grow, a growing network of local organizations, is bringing developmental screenings to
libraries, farmers markets and zoos instead of doctors' offices. Nationally only about 36% of
parents report completing a developmental screener in the previous 12 months; in New York the rate
is 28%. New York's Council on Children and Families announced a campaign to close the gap, with the
Adirondack Birth to Three Alliance participating.
