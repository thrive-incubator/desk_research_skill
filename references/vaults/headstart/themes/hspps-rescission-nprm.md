## Current snapshot

On August 6, 2026 the Office of Head Start filed a 200-page NPRM, *Reducing Federal Burden for
Head Start Programs* (RIN 0970-AD30, docket ACF-2026-0595), proposing to "rescind and replace" the
Head Start Program Performance Standards last revised in 2024, across 45 CFR Parts 1301, 1302,
1303, 1304 and 1305. It **published August 7, 2026**, and the comment period **closes October 6,
2026** — the date now confirmed on the Federal Register record, roughly five and a half weeks out
as of this week. This is the most positioning-relevant event the vault tracks: **every product sold
on "aligns with the HSPPS" is sold against a rulebook that may not exist in 2027**, and the window
to shape the replacement text is finite.

What the proposal does, in the filing's own terms: rescinds **more than 1,400 regulatory
provisions**; defers group size, ratios, background checks and transportation to **state** law;
rescinds **§ 1302.33 child screenings and assessments** (statutory screening and assessment duties
under the Head Start Act survive; the prescriptive federal implementation does not); drops
**CLASS: Pre-K as the named DRS instrument** and replaces the prescriptive school-readiness-goal
condition with "suitable results"; removes the **monthly mental health consultation** requirement
and the Health and Mental Health Services Advisory Committee; simplifies ERSEA, facilities and
reporting; broadens waiver authority (excluding nutrition, physical activity and eligibility); and
cuts the **administrative cost cap from 15% to 5%** of total approved program costs, with comment
sought on 10% as an alternative. It adds one new federal requirement: **30 minutes of physical
activity for every three and a half hours** of attendance, outdoors when weather permits, plus
"nutrient-dense, whole foods" within USDA CACFP meal standards.

The money is framed as reallocation rather than cut: $1,476,881,912 to $2,959,495,914 in annual
quantified impact at full implementation over 2027-2031 (primary estimate $2,218,188,913),
~$0.94 billion in annualized E.O. 14192 savings, and potential reinvestment into 116,516 Head
Start Preschool and 45,578 Early Head Start slots in 2031. ACF explicitly requested comment on
effective dates and implementation timing — the variable that decides whether a 2027 pilot
survives.

Entry-strategy read: re-anchor product claims on the **Head Start Act**, whose floor survives
intact; re-underwrite anything sold against a named federal instrument (CLASS prep, monthly IECMH
consultation, prescribed assessment cadences), because the buyer shifts from a compliance officer
to a program director with a budget and a choice; and build for **state variation**, since ratios,
background checks and transportation become fifty rulebooks instead of one. The 5% administrative
cap cuts both ways — it squeezes the line item most back-office software is bought from, while the
reinvestment story pushes dollars toward anything touching enrollment or child outcomes. Head
Start itself is not the political risk (87% voter support); the rulebook is. This is a distinct
rulemaking from the pending workforce final rule, and the two overlap
(see [[hspps-workforce-rule]], [[iecmh-financing-shift]]).

**Where it stands in the comment period's back half.** Two things changed in the August 20-27
window. First, the arc now has a **hard date**: comments close **October 6, 2026**. Second, the
sector acquired a working map of the proposal — FFYF published a **searchable section-by-section
guide** setting current citation against proposed change against remaining statutory basis, grouped
as Governance, Eligibility & Enrollment, Program Requirements, Health & Safety, and Curriculum &
Language, and documenting changes across §§ 1301-1303 that touch **group sizes, staff-child ratios,
home visitor caseloads, screening timelines and procedural requirements**. Those four parameters are
exactly what products get sized and priced against, which makes the guide the practical
repositioning worksheet for a vendor rather than another piece of commentary
(see [[ffyf]]).

Reaction also broadened rather than deepened. *The New Yorker* asked whether Head Start can be
"deregulated out of existence" — the first national-magazine framing the vault has recorded — while
coverage of local effects ran in **Arizona, Kentucky, Alabama, Wisconsin, Texas and southern
Maryland**, and **Montgomery County, Maryland** leaders formally opposed the changes. Arizona
providers characterized the proposal as cutting 90% of program standards; that figure is advocacy
framing carried by a local broadcaster, not an ACF count, and is recorded here as a measure of how
providers are reading the rule rather than as a fact about it. The operational consequence for
entry strategy is timing: executive directors and education managers are drafting comments and
briefing boards through early October, which is the worst window for a cold pilot pitch and a good
window for offering comment-drafting or impact-analysis help.

The counterweight this week was public opinion in a midterm battleground. An Ohio poll for
Groundwork Ohio and FFYF found **76%** of voters call finding and affording quality child care a
crisis or major problem and **70%** support a publicly funded program — **51% of Republicans** —
with **68%** calling federal early-learning funding a good investment. Read against the rescission,
the political economy is asymmetric: cross-party majorities support the program while the rulebook
is being withdrawn, which is why the fight is over standards rather than existence
(see [[enrollment-reduction-review]]). *(as of 2026-W35)*

### 2026-W35 — Ohio poll supplies the counterweight: 76% call child care a crisis, 70% back public funding
*First Five Years Fund / Groundwork Ohio · August 24, 2026* · [link](https://www.ffyf.org/2026/08/24/2026ohiopoll/)
UpONE Insights polled 600 registered Ohio voters August 3-6, 2026 (±4%) for Groundwork Ohio and
FFYF: 76% say finding and affording quality child care is a crisis or major problem (62% of

served by Ohio's Publicly Funded Child Care program; and 19% are putting off starting or expanding
a family because of costs. No Head Start-specific results.

### 2026-W32 — OHS proposes to rescind and replace the entire HSPPS

*Reducing Federal Burden for Head Start Programs* (RIN 0970-AD30, docket ACF-2026-0595) proposes
to rescind and replace the 2024 Performance Standards across 45 CFR Parts 1301-1305: more than
1,400 provisions rescinded, § 1302.33 child screenings and assessments rescinded, CLASS: Pre-K
removed as the named DRS instrument, monthly mental health consultation removed, administrative
cost cap cut from 15% to 5%, group size / ratios / background checks / transportation deferred to
states, and a new requirement of 30 minutes of physical activity per three and a half hours of
attendance. RIA: $1,476,881,912-$2,959,495,914 annual quantified impact at full implementation
(primary $2,218,188,913), ~$0.94 billion annualized E.O. 14192 savings, potential reinvestment
into 116,516 Head Start Preschool and 45,578 Early Head Start slots in 2031. ACF's own FY2025
figures behind the health cuts: $40,966,609 in health contractual services and $177,357,309 in
health/mental-health personnel; the 2024 monthly-consultation requirement was previously estimated
at ~$64 million annually.
