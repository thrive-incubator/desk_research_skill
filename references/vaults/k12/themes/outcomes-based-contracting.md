## Current snapshot

Outcomes-based contracting ties a portion of an education vendor's compensation to **measurable
results achieved for specific student groups**. W32 produced its first substantial efficacy evidence —
a **WestEd** study with the **Center for Outcomes Based Contracting** across eight districts in
California, Florida, Mississippi and Texas over 2022–25, in which **three of the eight showed positive
causal impacts** at **comparable spend to traditional contracts** — and completed contracts had grown
from 5 to 46 since 2022 with 87 school systems participating.

**W34 gives the model a federal endorsement.** [[us-department-of-education]]'s edtech Dear Colleague
Letter instructs states and districts to **incorporate evidence of effectiveness into procurement,
especially products that have conducted randomized controlled trials**, to evaluate tools on outcomes
and instructional value rather than adoption, logins or screen time, and to establish edtech
implementation and outcome reviews. It explicitly **praises Arkansas, Indiana, Louisiana, Michigan and
Texas for "exploring contracting models" that rely more on "performance measures" and less on
"product adoption or usage metrics."** Assistant Secretary Kirsten Baesler framed responsible design
as "the floor," writing that "not only do we need to know if the tool works, but if it works; why does
it work, for whom does it work, and under what conditions." The letter asks vendors to publish
independent evaluations "whenever feasible" and to improve continuously using classroom evidence.

Two caveats now attach. First, on **what counts as evidence**: Helen Crompton warned against defining
it too narrowly — "an RCT is not the only meaningful form of evidence. The same technology can produce
very different results depending on the teacher, subject, age group, amount of training, and how it is
integrated into instruction." Second, on **whether outcomes actually protect a program**: the
[[teen-pregnancy-prevention-program]] terminations killed HOPE Buffalo, credited with a **36% decline
in teen pregnancy rates** in high-poverty Buffalo neighborhoods, on priority alignment rather than
performance. Proving outcomes is becoming necessary to *get bought*; W34 shows it is not sufficient to
*stay funded*.

For a wellbeing vendor that can measure, this remains the most favorable procurement development in
the vault — the rare channel where "it works" converts directly into "it gets bought." The federal
guidance carries no force of regulation, but it gives a district's procurement office written cover to
demand evidence.

## Timeline

### 2026-W34 — ED guidance endorses performance-based contracting by description, naming five states
*EdSurge · August 20, 2026* · [link](https://edsurge.com/news/department-of-education-issues-long-awaited-edtech-guidance-for-states-and-districts)
ED's edtech Dear Colleague Letter tells states and districts to incorporate evidence of effectiveness into procurement, "especially products that have conducted randomized controlled trials," to evaluate edtech on outcomes rather than adoption or screen time, and to establish implementation and outcome reviews. It praised Arkansas, Indiana, Louisiana, Michigan and Texas for "exploring contracting models" relying on "performance measures" rather than "product adoption or usage metrics." Helen Crompton of the Research Institute of Digital Innovation in Learning cautioned against defining evidence too narrowly, noting "an RCT is not the only meaningful form of evidence."

### 2026-W33 — Hechinger tracks the spread of paying vendors for student results
*Hechinger Report · August 10, 2026* · [link](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-outcomes-basedcontracting/)
Hechinger's Proof Points column examined outcomes-based contracting — arrangements in which vendor
payment is tied to measured student results — and its spread among districts. Published the same
week a major virtual tutoring provider shut down with experts citing insufficient evidence of
effectiveness (also: [The 74, August 10, 2026](https://www.the74million.org/article/major-virtual-tutoring-provider-shuts-down-experts-cite-lack-of-evidence/)).

### 2026-W32 — WestEd finds early causal evidence that outcomes-based contracting works
*K-12 Dive · August 4, 2026* · [link](https://www.k12dive.com/news/early-evidence-finds-outcomes-based-contracting-boosts-student-performance/826856/)
WestEd, partnering with the Center for Outcomes Based Contracting, studied eight districts across
California, Florida, Mississippi and Texas during the 2022–25 school years, interviewing seven
district leaders, six tutoring providers, three ed tech vendors and 12 school leaders. Three
districts showed positive causal impacts on student performance, including large proficiency gains
for 2nd graders using an AI-based English language arts platform, substantial increases in students
achieving typical reading growth targets through literacy tutoring, and strong gains from math
tutoring. Districts spent comparable amounts under outcomes-based contracts versus traditional ones.
Completed outcomes-based contracts grew from 5 to 46 between 2022 and 2025, with 87 school systems
now participating
(also: [The 74 opinion, August 6](https://www.the74million.org/article/accountability-works-outcomes-based-contracting-makes-it-work-better/)).
