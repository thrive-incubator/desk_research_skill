## Current snapshot

Roughly **1,400 districts** are suing Meta, Snap, YouTube, and TikTok over social-media harm to
students, and the money has started to land: Kentucky's **Breathitt County Schools settled for
$27M in May 2026 — more than its $22M annual budget**. Six bellwether cases are being tracked to
forecast outcomes for the rest, with the next trials (Tucson Unified, AZ and Charleston County,
SC) set for **February 8, 2027**. What makes this arc matter to a wellbeing organization is not
just the size but the shape: the money is district-controlled, carries no federal strings, has an
explicit wellbeing purpose, and already has an organized intermediary shaping how it gets spent —
the Children's Funding Project's "Fund In Real Life" initiative, which is directing districts
toward child-wellbeing activities and calls the payouts "once-in-a-generation funding." It is
arriving precisely as federal wellbeing grants contract (see
[[school-mental-health-funding]]). The emerging spend model is structured real-world activity
rather than screening-and-referral software: Spokane Public Schools pre-invested $600K in
FY2024-25 and $500K in 2025-26 in an "Engage in Real Life" program running 284 after-school
activities at 65% student participation, targeting 75% by 2028.
*(as of 2026-W31)*
