## Current snapshot

Public education dollars are being redirected to households and to new categories of operator through
three parallel channels. W32 advanced two — first data on **Texas's inaugural voucher/ESA program**,
the largest new state entrant, and litigation over a **Jewish charter school in Oklahoma** testing
whether religious organizations can operate publicly funded charters — while the federal
[[education-freedom-tax-credit]] launches January 1, 2027 with roughly 9 in 10 students eligible.

**W34 establishes how hard these programs are to regulate once universal.** The **Arizona Supreme
Court** invalidated enough signatures to keep the **Protect Education Act** — which would have imposed
significant oversight and an income cap on the state's Empowerment Scholarship Accounts — off the
November ballot, and the state teachers union conceded. Arizona opened ESAs to all families four years
ago and is described as the movement's proving ground; EdChoice president Robert Enlow said Arizona is
"way out in front of everyone else" and that guardrails risk reversing that. The fight split the
choice movement: the **American Federation for Children**, once chaired by Betsy DeVos, backed a
competing compromise measure and accused other pro-voucher groups of "playing Russian roulette" with
children's futures, arguing it was "wrong and reckless to risk 100,000 scholarships."

Oversight now moves to the ballot in a different form: state superintendent **Tom Horne**, who rejected
some ESA purchases as non-educational, lost the July Republican primary to Treasurer **Kimberly Yee**,
who said such calls belong to lawmakers — in the most expensive state superintendent race to date. Yee
faces Democrat **Teresa Ruiz** in November, who says ESA management is the "number one" topic voters
raise. Two parallel retreats reinforce the pattern: **Florida** Republicans abandoned voucher-oversight
legislation in 2025 after an audit flagged unaccounted millions, and **West Virginia** did not advance
cost-containment legislation; in Iowa's GOP gubernatorial primary, Rep. Randy Feenstra was accused of
betrayal for raising concerns and lost despite a Trump endorsement.

For a wellbeing vendor the read-through is a market-access one. A growing pool of public dollars is
flowing through channels with limited spend oversight and **no district procurement gate at all** —
ESA-eligible direct-to-family purchasing is a real and largely unexamined route to market, and the
W34 result suggests the oversight that would constrain it is not arriving soon.
*(as of 2026-W34)*
