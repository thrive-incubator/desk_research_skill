## Current snapshot

**The IDEA transfer is a completed fact being publicly defended, not a proposal.** Secretary Linda
McMahon defended the reorganization at the August 4 OSEP conference; under the June 2026 interagency
agreement, OSERS moved the formula and discretionary grant program components for **IDEA Parts B, C and
D to [[hhs]]** while ED retains legal responsibility for the underlying education law — splitting money
administration from legal authority. It is one of **14 interagency agreements ED has signed with six
federal agencies**. Congressional resistance keeps building and keeps arriving late: bipartisan Senate
bills would reverse the breakup, but all exempt agreements already in effect February 1, 2025, which
protects the executed transfer.

**W34 shows the same devolution running through guidance rather than reorganization.** ED's edtech
Dear Colleague Letter **expressly declined to issue rules and delegated implementation to states and
districts** — described in the trade press as a "laissez-faire federal approach." Helen Crompton's
objection names the stake precisely: "I would like to see a national floor, but not necessarily a
national ceiling. There are some expectations that should not depend on which state a student happens
to live in. Student privacy, accessibility, transparency, responsible use of student data, and basic
protections around the use of educational technologies are examples where greater national consistency
is valuable." Digital Promise's Jean-Claude Brizard added that when technology rules vary widely, "we
inadvertently widen equity gaps." On civil rights, OCR's discipline letter continues the same
direction — withdrawing federal outcome-based scrutiny and recasting it as itself a Title VI violation.

The parallel pattern outside ED is now large enough to name. [[hhs]]'s [[head-start]] proposed rule
would replace detailed federal performance standards with compliance to **state** licensing law across
1,600 grantees. And the [[fcc]] is asking whether [[e-rate]] "should be limited or sunset." Three
different agencies, one direction: **the federal floor is being lowered or removed, and the resulting
variation is being pushed down to states and districts.**

For a wellbeing vendor this is the structural fact that matters most across the whole vault. A federal
floor creates one compliance market with one specification; fifty state regimes create a fragmented
one where the sales motion is per-state and the product spec is negotiable. Every arc the vault tracks
— screening mandates, mental health consultation, discipline analytics, connectivity — is moving from
the first condition to the second.

## Timeline

### 2026-W34 — ED declines to write edtech rules, delegating to states and districts
*EdSurge · August 20, 2026* · [link](https://edsurge.com/news/department-of-education-issues-long-awaited-edtech-guidance-for-states-and-districts)
ED's edtech Dear Colleague Letter offered no new rules and "expressly delegated implementation to states and districts." Helen Crompton of the Research Institute of Digital Innovation in Learning objected that student privacy, accessibility, transparency, responsible use of student data and basic protections "should not depend on which state a student happens to live in," calling for "a national floor, but not necessarily a national ceiling"; Digital Promise's Jean-Claude Brizard warned that widely varying technology rules "inadvertently widen equity gaps."

### 2026-W34 — Head Start rule would substitute state licensing law for federal performance standards
*The 74 / The 19th · August 16, 2026* · [link](https://www.the74million.org/zero2eight/head-start-could-soon-be-overhauled-heres-exactly-what-would-change/)
The ACF proposed rule would have Head Start "operate much more like other childcare centers under state and local rules," eliminating detailed federal requirements on facilities, safety training, hygiene, disaster preparedness and equipment and instructing programs to follow state law instead. Katie Hamm, who oversaw Head Start under the Biden administration, said states "didn't design their childcare programs to serve the population that Head Start serves… not to prepare a high-need community for school."

### 2026-W32 — McMahon defends the completed IDEA transfer to HHS at the OSEP conference
*K-12 Dive · August 4, 2026* · [link](https://www.k12dive.com/news/education-department-promotes-special-education-shakeup-OSEP-conference/826981/)
Education Secretary Linda McMahon defended the special-education reorganization at the OSEP
conference on August 4. Under a June 2026 interagency agreement, OSERS moved formula and
discretionary grant program components for IDEA Parts B, C and D to HHS while ED retains legal
responsibility for the education laws; ED now holds 14 interagency agreements with six federal
agencies. Special educators, disability-rights advocates and some members of Congress said the
arrangement creates confusion and reduces accountability for compliance.

### 2026-W32 — New bipartisan Senate bill would reverse the ED breakup
*Chalkbeat · July 31, 2026* · [link](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2026/07/31/trump-break-up-education-department-reversed-senate-bipartisan-bill/)
A new bipartisan Senate bill would reverse the administration's moves to dismantle the Education
Department and distribute its functions across other agencies.

### 2026-W31 — Bipartisan Senate bills would bar further ED transfers, but exempt existing agreements
*K-12 Dive · July 23, 2026* · [link](https://www.k12dive.com/news/2-senate-bills-would-undo-some-education-department-transfers/826026/)
S. 5046 — Kaine (D-VA), Collins (R-ME), Murkowski (R-AK) — would prohibit transferring the ED
offices overseeing special education, postsecondary, elementary and secondary, and Native American
education, covering interagency agreements and contracting authorities to prevent circumvention. A
separate bill from Cassidy (R-LA) would bar outsourcing special-education functions to HHS, an
agreement described as especially contentious over its effect on services for children with
disabilities. Both bills exempt interagency agreements already in effect February 1, 2025
(also: [K-12 Dive, July 27](https://www.k12dive.com/news/week-in-review-7-27-26/826193/)).

### 2026-W30 — Special educators oppose IDEA move to HHS; FY27 would cut OSERS staff 163 → 31
*K-12 Dive · July 21, 2026* · [link](https://www.k12dive.com/news/special-educators-oppose-education-department-breakup-interagency-agreement-Health-Human-Services/825821/)
At the Special Education Legislative Summit, special-education professionals and former ED
officials opposed the interagency agreement moving IDEA grants, compliance and monitoring, and
state performance determinations to HHS — one of 14 agreements ED has signed with six agencies.
86 House Democrats asked Secretary McMahon to reverse it, House Republicans advanced a 10-bill
codification package, and ED's proposed FY27 budget would cut OSERS staff from 163 to 31; IDEA
funds $15.5B in FY26 for roughly 8.2 million students.
