## Current snapshot

The classroom-AI land-grab is colliding with newly forming procurement gates, and in W34 **the gate
acquired a federal endorsement and the harm case acquired a dataset**. On the procurement side,
[[us-department-of-education]] issued edtech guidance telling states and districts to evaluate tools
on outcomes rather than logins or screen time and to **incorporate evidence of effectiveness into
procurement, especially products that have conducted randomized controlled trials** — praising
Arkansas, Indiana, Louisiana, Michigan and Texas for performance-based contracting models. It
declined to write rules, delegating to states and districts; Helen Crompton of the Research Institute
of Digital Innovation in Learning warned against defining evidence too narrowly ("an RCT is not the
only meaningful form of evidence") and argued for "a national floor, but not necessarily a national
ceiling" on privacy, accessibility and data use.

Against that, the available evidence is not flattering. Two new research benchmarks landed:
**EduClaw-Bench**, evaluating agent configurations over a **30-day learning horizon** with simulated
learners built from real knowledge-tracing data, found **almost no model or agent combination —
including GPT-5.5 and Alibaba's Qwen models — maintained strong tutoring performance over time**,
concluding that sustained tutoring needs learner modeling, orchestration and "pedagogical memory."
**ELBench** evaluated nine models across capability, safety and two education modules and found the
**two education-specialized models failed to lead in either education-specific module**. EdTech
Insiders notes most edtech companies are not benchmarking against any research-released benchmark at
all. Reach Capital's Jennifer Carolan, by contrast, said she had changed her mind on how useful AI
tutors are becoming.

And the student-side harm case is now quantified rather than anecdotal. [[common-sense-media]]'s
nationally representative survey of 1,017 teens found **70% use AI for schoolwork**, **38% say it
leads them to develop fewer original ideas**, 39% feel they are missing out on learning, and 63% go
straight to a chatbot for an answer. Only **30% have ever had a teacher discuss safe use**. School
blocking measurably fails: of the 44% who hit a block, **59% moved to a personal device with less
oversight**. Common Sense shipped a Digital Literacy & Well-Being Curriculum and an AI and Student
Well-Being PD course the same day.

The synthesis for a wellbeing product: the federal government now says buy on evidence, the research
says single-turn quality does not predict 30-day performance and "education-specialized" confers no
measured advantage, and the most-cited teen dataset frames AI as a **wellbeing** problem rather than
an integrity one. Durability over weeks, not demo quality, is the thing to be able to prove — and
the wellbeing framing is what routes the purchase to a wellbeing budget.
*(as of 2026-W34)*

## Timeline

### 2026-W34 — ED guidance tells districts to buy edtech on RCT evidence, not usage
*EdSurge · August 20, 2026* · [link](https://edsurge.com/news/department-of-education-issues-long-awaited-edtech-guidance-for-states-and-districts)
Assistant Secretary Kirsten Baesler's Dear Colleague Letter directs states and districts to distinguish "recreational" from "instructional" technology, evaluate tools on outcomes rather than screen time, and incorporate evidence of effectiveness into procurement, "especially products that have conducted randomized controlled trials," while declining to issue federal rules. It praised Arkansas, Indiana, Louisiana, Michigan and Texas for performance-based contracting models and asked vendors to publish independent evaluations "whenever feasible."

### 2026-W34 — Two benchmarks find AI tutors degrade over 30 days and education-specialized models don't lead
*EdTech Insiders · August 13, 2026* · [link](https://edtechinsiders.substack.com/p/new-tutoring-benchmarks-continue)
EduClaw-Bench evaluated AI-agent configurations over a 30-day learning horizon using simulated learners from real knowledge-tracing data and found almost no model or agent combination studied, including GPT-5.5 and Alibaba's Qwen models, maintained strong tutoring performance over time — pointing to a need for learner modeling, orchestration and "pedagogical memory." ELBench evaluated nine models and found the two education-specialized systems failed to lead in either education-specific module. Most edtech companies are not benchmarking their products against any research-released benchmark.

### 2026-W34 — Common Sense Media: 70% of teens use AI for schoolwork, 38% say it costs them original ideas
*The 74 · August 18, 2026* · [link](https://www.the74million.org/article/survey-5-ways-ai-is-cutting-into-students-ability-to-learn/)
A nationally representative survey of 1,017 U.S. teens aged 13-17 fielded April-May 2026 found 70% use AI for schoolwork, only 30% had ever discussed safe use with a teacher and 26% how to evaluate accuracy, 63% go straight to a chatbot for an answer, 38% say AI leads them to develop fewer original ideas and 39% feel they are missing out on learning. Of the 44% who had a tool blocked at school, 59% switched to a personal device with less oversight. Common Sense launched a Digital Literacy & Well-Being Curriculum and an AI and Student Well-Being PD course alongside the findings.
