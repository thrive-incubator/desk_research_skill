## Current snapshot

**The state channel is now visibly outperforming the federal one, and W34 is the sharpest side-by-side
the vault has recorded.** Federally, the picture is subtraction: the temporary restraining order
preserving ED's school mental-health grants **expires August 24, 2026** with only a preliminary
injunction between the money and termination; the [[teen-pregnancy-prevention-program]] terminations
reached named grantees, costing three New York organizations **over $4.3 million a year** serving
**19,000+ young people**; and the [[head-start]] proposed rule would delete **the monthly mental
health consultation requirement** across 1,600 grantees.

Against that, **Pennsylvania made $140 million available for school safety and student mental health
grants** — the largest sum moving *toward* student mental health anywhere in the W34 scan, and a state
program rather than a federal one. (Both reports of it reached the vault via Google News aggregation;
the headline figure and purpose are consistent across the two, but program eligibility and
application detail could not be retrieved and should be confirmed against the solicitation.)

A second, smaller state-and-local channel is now legible: **local ballot measures**. Denver's **$44
million mill levy override** carries an explicit **$2.9 million to expand student mental health
support** plus $2 million for special education, on a November 3 ballot — and Denver's 2020 override
funded the same categories and passed. Mill levy money is recurring, locally controlled, and legally
restricted to educational programming, which is a materially better durability profile than anything
currently flowing through ED. See [[local-tax-referendums]].

W34 also delivers a rare look at **what districts actually buy** when they have the money. Oceanside
Unified in California assembled a three-vendor multi-tiered stack at a 700-student school with a
single counselor: [[move-this-world]] for Tier 1 classroom curriculum that generates referrals,
[[riverside-insights]]' DESSA for triage and progress monitoring, and [[stepping-stones-group]] for
clinical delivery. That configuration — referral generation, triage, contracted capacity — is the
shape of demand, and the handoff to clinical capacity is where it either works or becomes a waitlist.
Supporting context: a KFF analysis found **58% of public schools reported more students seeking
school-based mental health support in 2024-25** than the prior year, while just half said they could
effectively serve all students with social and emotional needs.

The durability test from W32 keeps holding: money written into statute or a bargained contract
outlasts money that is discretionary, transitional, or dependent on agency priorities.
*(as of 2026-W34)*
