## Current snapshot

**The infrastructure every cloud-delivered wellbeing product depends on is now formally under review
for termination, and the review has been fused to the school screen-time fight.** [[e-rate]] is the
**fifth-largest pot of federal funds schools receive** — **$10.5 billion to districts between 2021
and 2025**, and **$13.2 billion to nearly 25,000 entities** in total over that period, most of it to
districts. The [[fcc]] published a proposed rule on **August 14, 2026** asking whether the program
"should be limited or sunset" given "today's extensive connectivity rates," and whether policy
changes are needed in light of screen-time concerns in schools. The rule also floats conditioning
E-rate support on schools giving parents **"a meaningful opportunity to opt their children out of
screen-based instruction or screen use during the school day."** Comment closes **October 13, 2026**.
The Education and Libraries Network Coalition — co-chaired by AASA, the American Library Association
and the Bernstein Strategy Group — is organizing against it, arguing the program funds only eligible
services and equipment, not devices or content, so "questions about curriculum, instructional
practice, or student behavior should not be used to undermine a proven program." AASA planned a
virtual town hall for September 9, 2026.

Two distinct risks for a wellbeing vendor, and the smaller-sounding one is the nearer. Outright
sunset would remove the connectivity subsidy underneath every telehealth session, screener, and SEL
platform delivered over a district network. But the **parental opt-out condition** would bite sooner
and harder: a district obliged to offer opt-outs from screen-based instruction becomes a structurally
worse buyer for anything delivered on a device, regardless of what the tool does. That makes the FCC
a third venue — alongside ED guidance and state legislatures — deciding the
[[screen-time-device-policy]] question, and the only one of the three with a funding lever attached.

Historical scale for context: 8% of public school classrooms had internet access in 1995, the year
before the Telecommunications Act created the program; 93% did by 2003.
*(as of 2026-W34)*
