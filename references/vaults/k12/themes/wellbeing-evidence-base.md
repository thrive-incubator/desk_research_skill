## Current snapshot

The research base that legitimizes school wellbeing spending took a significant hit in W31 — **Ron Avi
Astor (UCLA) publicly retracted the causal claim** that SEL, restorative-justice and trauma-informed
investment prevent school shootings ("We were wrong," at the April 2026 AERA meeting), removing the
argument that sold many of those programs to school boards without showing the programs fail at what
they actually do.

**W34 is the week the evidence question turned procedural: the demand for evidence, the supply of it,
and the question of whether it protects anything all moved at once.**

**Demand.** [[us-department-of-education]] told states and districts to put evidence of effectiveness
into procurement, "especially products that have conducted randomized controlled trials." That is the
strongest federal statement yet that efficacy is a purchasing criterion — with the caveat from Helen
Crompton that "an RCT is not the only meaningful form of evidence," since the same technology performs
differently by teacher, subject, age group, training and integration.

**Supply, and it is thin.** Two new benchmarks found AI tutors do not hold up: **EduClaw-Bench** showed
almost no model or agent configuration — GPT-5.5 and Qwen included — **maintained strong tutoring
performance across a 30-day horizon**, and **ELBench** found **education-specialized models failed to
lead in either education-specific module**. Most edtech companies benchmark against none of this.
The practical lesson is that short-horizon evaluation systematically overstates what a product will do
over a term.

**Whether evidence protects you — apparently not.** The [[teen-pregnancy-prevention-program]]
terminations killed HOPE Buffalo despite a documented **36% decline in teen pregnancy rates** in
high-poverty Buffalo neighborhoods from 2015 to 2020, on grounds of priority alignment. Evidence is
becoming necessary to get bought and demonstrably insufficient to stay funded.

**New this week: reimbursement, not evidence, is often the binding constraint.** Developmental
screening has been recommended by the American Academy of Pediatrics since 2006 at 9, 18 and 30
months, yet only **36% of parents nationally** and **28% in New York** report completing a screener in
the past year. Practitioners named the cause plainly — Noelle Hause of Zero to Three: "Pediatric
clinics often lose money on screening… it's not because it's not recommended, it's because it's not
getting reimbursed." New York's Medicaid rate is **under $20 per screening**. A mandated, evidence-
backed practice fails at the payment layer, which is a different failure mode from Astor's and one no
amount of additional efficacy research would fix.

**Two datasets on the same wellbeing gap.** [[common-sense-media]] quantified student-reported
cognitive costs of AI (**38%** fewer original ideas, **39%** feeling they are missing out on learning),
reframing AI from an integrity issue to a wellbeing one. And GLISTEN and the Trevor Project found
affirming school environments correlate with higher grades, belonging and participation, while **more
than half** of trans and nonbinary students reported at least one discriminatory school policy and
roughly **60%** at least one negative event — a finding echoed by NYC's own survey, where bathroom
access matching gender identity ran at **86%** overall but **65%** for students identified as neither
male nor female.
*(as of 2026-W34)*

## Timeline

### 2026-W34 — Benchmarks show AI tutoring degrades over 30 days; education-specialized models don't lead
*EdTech Insiders · August 13, 2026* · [link](https://edtechinsiders.substack.com/p/new-tutoring-benchmarks-continue)
EduClaw-Bench evaluated AI-agent configurations over a 30-day learning horizon using simulated learners built from real knowledge-tracing data and found almost no model or agent combination — including GPT-5.5 and Alibaba's Qwen models — maintained strong tutoring performance over time, pointing to a need for learner modeling, orchestration and "pedagogical memory." ELBench evaluated nine models and found the two education-specialized systems failed to lead in either education-specific module. Most edtech companies are not benchmarking against any research-released benchmark.

### 2026-W34 — Developmental screening fails on reimbursement, not evidence: 28% in New York, under $20 a screen
*Hechinger Report · August 17, 2026* · [link](https://hechingerreport.org/in-new-york-most-kids-miss-recommended-screening-for-autism-speech-delays/)
Just over 36% of parents of young children nationally, and about 28% in New York, report completing a developmental screener in the previous 12 months, despite the American Academy of Pediatrics recommending standardized screening at 9, 18 and 30 months since 2006. "Pediatric clinics often lose money on screening," said Noelle Hause of Zero to Three — "it's not because it's not recommended, it's because it's not getting reimbursed." New York's Medicaid rate remains under $20 per screening; the State Health Department issued a memo citing the low rate and launched a "Be Their Biggest Champion" campaign. Help Me Grow operates in New York and 29 other states; documented screenings in Onondaga County rose thirtyfold, from about 150 to more than 4,600 annually, since 2019.

### 2026-W34 — Common Sense Media quantifies the cognitive cost teens attribute to AI
*The 74 · August 18, 2026* · [link](https://www.the74million.org/article/survey-5-ways-ai-is-cutting-into-students-ability-to-learn/)
In a nationally representative survey of 1,017 teens aged 13-17, 38% said having AI available leads them to develop fewer original ideas and 39% said they feel they are missing out on learning when they use it to complete assignments; 70% use AI for schoolwork while only 30% had ever discussed safe use with a teacher. Asked which skills matter most for their future, teens chose working well with others (55%), fact-checking and critical thinking (50%), and reading and understanding complex information (48%).

### 2026-W34 — GLISTEN and Trevor Project link affirming school environments to grades and belonging
*The 74 · August 20, 2026* · [link](https://www.the74million.org/article/higher-grades-and-belonging-trans-students-thrive-when-supported-by-teachers/)
A report from GLISTEN and the Trevor Project found transgender and nonbinary students whose identities are affirmed at school report greater belonging, higher grades and fuller class participation, with similar outcomes where they can connect with LGBTQ+ peers, see positive representation, or attend schools with inclusive policies. More than half said their school had at least one policy or practice discriminatory toward LGBTQ+ students and roughly 60% reported at least one negative school event — 49% verbal harassment related to their identity, 27% being barred from dressing in accordance with their gender.

### 2026-W33 — A major virtual tutoring provider shuts down over thin efficacy evidence
*The 74 · August 10, 2026* · [link](https://www.the74million.org/article/major-virtual-tutoring-provider-shuts-down-experts-cite-lack-of-evidence/)
A large virtual tutoring provider closed, with experts attributing the failure in part to
insufficient evidence of effectiveness. In the same week, new benchmarks showed continued gaps in AI
models' performance on teaching and learning tasks
(also: [EdTech Insiders, August 13, 2026](https://edtechinsiders.substack.com/p/new-tutoring-benchmarks-continue)),
and reading scores continued to drop for many middle schoolers
(also: [EdSurge, August 10, 2026](https://edsurge.com/news/reading-scores-continue-to-drop-for-many-middle-schoolers-heres-why)).

### 2026-W32 — WestEd finds early causal evidence for outcomes-based contracting
*K-12 Dive · August 4, 2026* · [link](https://www.k12dive.com/news/early-evidence-finds-outcomes-based-contracting-boosts-student-performance/826856/)
WestEd, with the Center for Outcomes Based Contracting, studied eight districts across California,
Florida, Mississippi and Texas over 2022–25. Three districts showed positive causal impacts on
student performance, including large 2nd-grade proficiency gains on an AI-based ELA platform and
substantial increases in students hitting typical reading growth targets. Districts spent comparable
amounts to traditional contracts. Completed outcomes-based contracts grew from 5 to 46 between 2022
and 2025, with 87 school systems participating.

### 2026-W31 — Leading school-shootings researcher retracts the bullying-and-climate theory
*Hechinger Report · July 27, 2026* · [link](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-school-shootings/)
Ron Avi Astor (UCLA), a leading school-violence scholar, reversed decades of his own position at
the April 2026 AERA meeting: "We were wrong." His theory held that shooters were driven by local
grievances — bullying, trauma, school climate — and that school-based intervention could prevent
shootings, a claim that drove heavy district investment in SEL, restorative justice, and
trauma-informed care. He now points to weapon-carrying among California students declining
steadily over 25 years while national school-shooting incidents rose, argues perpetrators seek
national notoriety, and recommends media and platform coverage guidelines rather than school-based
prevention alone.
