## Current snapshot

Special education is the one place in K12 where documented unmet student need converts *legally* into a
spending obligation, which makes it the most reliable observed demand signal the vault has for
contracted student services. W33 opened with formal IDEA state complaints jumping **nearly 50%** and
New York City still missing most deadlines on legally mandated special education orders — demand a
district cannot decline.

W34 adds the **staffing** dimension and the **early-childhood** risk.

**Staffing.** New York City has a shortage of roughly **1,600 paraprofessionals** — the aides who
typically support students with disabilities, starting at about **$33,000 a year** and among the
lowest-paid school employees. The UFT argues vacancies have severe consequences, including students
with disabilities who cannot ride school buses without an aide. A City Council bill guaranteeing a
one-time **$10,000 "respect check"** passed **49-0** and became law August 19 when Mayor Zohran Mamdani
declined to sign or veto; City Hall immediately sued, arguing it bypasses collective bargaining and
sets a "dangerous precedent." Total cost: about **$325 million** across roughly **25,000**
paraprofessionals, in four installments between January and August 2027. Some paraprofessionals object
that one-time payments don't count toward pensions. The case is a natural experiment in whether
districts close aide shortages with pay, agency contracting, or technology — and its legal outcome
determines whether legislatures can mandate school labor costs outside bargaining.

**Money on a ballot.** Denver's **$44 million** mill levy override includes **$2 million specifically
for special education services**, with the district citing "an increase in the number of students
needing special education services" as a driver of financial strain (see [[local-tax-referendums]]).

**Early-childhood risk.** The [[head-start]] proposed rule would require programs to comply with
applicable federal and state regulations for children with disabilities **but eliminates much of the
structure for how to provide those services** — removing the specified mechanism while nominally
keeping the obligation. Combined with the removal of the expulsion prohibition and the screening
timelines, the effect is to convert structured early-intervention pathways into program discretion
across 1,600 grantees.

The through-line is unchanged and strengthening: legal obligation plus in-house capacity shortfall is
what pushes districts toward contracted providers and teletherapy — the demand [[stepping-stones-group]]
and comparable staffing vendors serve.
*(as of 2026-W34)*

## Timeline

### 2026-W34 — NYC's 1,600-paraprofessional shortage produces a $325M mandated pay bump and a lawsuit
*Chalkbeat · August 19, 2026* · [link](https://www.chalkbeat.org/newyork/2026/08/19/nyc-paraprofessionals-10000-pay-bump-law-mamdani/)
A bill guaranteeing New York City paraprofessionals a one-time $10,000 payment became law August 19 after Mayor Zohran Mamdani declined to sign or veto it; City Hall announced a legal challenge within hours, arguing it improperly sidesteps collective bargaining. Paraprofessionals, who typically support students with disabilities, start at roughly $33,000 a year. The Council passed it 49-0 and estimates about $325 million in total across roughly 25,000 eligible paraprofessionals, paid in four installments between January and August 2027. The UFT argues the raises address a shortage of roughly 1,600 paraprofessionals, vacancies that can prevent students with disabilities from riding school buses (also: [The 74, August 20, 2026](https://www.the74million.org/article/nyc-mayor-sues-city-council-over-law-giving-10000-bonuses-to-paraprofessionals/)).

### 2026-W34 — Denver ballot measure carries $2M for special education as service demand rises
*Chalkbeat · August 14, 2026* · [link](https://www.chalkbeat.org/colorado/2026/08/14/denver-voters-to-decide-on-44-million-mill-levy-override-for-public-schools/)
Denver's $44 million mill levy override, placed on the November 3 ballot by a unanimous board vote, includes $2 million for special education services and $2.9 million for student mental health. The district cited an increase in the number of students needing special education services — alongside declining enrollment under per-student funding and rising health insurance and utility costs — as a source of financial strain.

### 2026-W34 — Head Start rule keeps the disability obligation but removes the structure for meeting it
*The 74 / The 19th · August 16, 2026* · [link](https://www.the74million.org/zero2eight/head-start-could-soon-be-overhauled-heres-exactly-what-would-change/)
The proposed rule requires Head Start programs to comply with all applicable federal and state regulations in providing services to children with disabilities but "eliminates much of the structure for how to provide those services." It also removes the prohibition on expelling children and the fixed timelines for vision, hearing, immunization and well-child screening, converting specified early-intervention pathways into program discretion across 1,600 grant recipients.

### 2026-W33 — IDEA state complaints jump nearly 50% as NYC keeps missing service deadlines
