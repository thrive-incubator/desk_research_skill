## Current snapshot

Removing a student from school is the outcome most school wellbeing interventions are ultimately sold
against, which makes how exclusion is counted a commercial question and not only a moral one. W33
opened the arc with a warning about **measurement** — Massachusetts suspensions falling nearly 15 years
after reform with evidence that emergency removals may be absorbing part of the decline (substitution,
not reduction), and Philadelphia parents describing the toll of preschool exclusion.

**W34 removes the measurement itself.** [[us-department-of-education]]'s Office for Civil Rights issued
discipline guidance telling schools to "stop racial balancing" and stating that **"it is race
discrimination under Title VI for the school to evaluate the racial outcomes of their discipline
policies and to make decisions based on or because of those racial outcomes."** Assistant Secretary for
Civil Rights Kimberly Richey wrote that neither Title VI nor the Constitution prohibits unintentional
disparate impact. The letter follows the administration's formal rescission of the disparate-impact
theory the prior month, and says only intentional discrimination is prohibited — a standard former
federal civil-rights attorneys describe as far harder to investigate and prove. Disparate-outcome data
may still be evidence but "cannot substitute for the ultimate inquiry."

The commercial consequence is direct and negative for one product category. Disparate-impact review was
the compliance hook that sold discipline analytics: districts bought dashboards partly to detect and
defend against OCR exposure. That driver is not merely gone — outcome review is now recast as itself a
legal risk. The underlying need has not changed; the *buying justification* has, and any product whose
pitch is "find your discipline disparities" needs a different one.

At the other end of the age range, exclusion is being *enabled*. The [[head-start]] proposed rule would
**remove the prohibition on expelling children from Head Start**, letting programs set their own
disciplinary rules within state and local licensing. The rule text says this is "not an endorsement of
suspension and expulsion" and programs would still owe children with behavioral concerns screenings and
referrals — but the floor that made preschool expulsion effectively unavailable would be gone, in the
age band where W33 already documented the harm.

## Timeline

### 2026-W34 — OCR declares outcome-based discipline review itself a Title VI violation
*K-12 Dive · August 18, 2026* · [link](https://www.k12dive.com/news/ocr-calls-on-schools-to-stop-racial-balancing-in-new-discipline-guidance/828211/)
ED's Office for Civil Rights told districts to "stop racial balancing," stating that "it is race discrimination under Title VI for the school to evaluate the racial outcomes of their discipline policies and to make decisions based on or because of those racial outcomes." Assistant Secretary Kimberly Richey wrote that neither Title VI nor the Constitution prohibits unintentional disparate impact. The letter follows the administration's formal undoing of the disparate-impact theory the previous month; former federal civil-rights attorneys note intentional-discrimination cases are much harder to prove, and the letter says disparate-outcome data "cannot substitute for the ultimate inquiry."

### 2026-W34 — Head Start proposed rule would remove the prohibition on expelling children
*The 74 / The 19th · August 16, 2026* · [link](https://www.the74million.org/zero2eight/head-start-could-soon-be-overhauled-heres-exactly-what-would-change/)
Children generally cannot currently be expelled from Head Start; the proposed rule would remove that prohibition and allow programs to set their own disciplinary rules so long as they align with state and local requirements, while still requiring screenings and referral services for children with behavioral problems. The administration said the 2016 rules were written when many states lacked their own policies, and that the change is "not an endorsement of suspension and expulsion as approaches to address persistent and serious behavioral concerns."
