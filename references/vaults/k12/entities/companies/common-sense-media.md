## Current snapshot

**Common Sense Media is doing both halves of the influence loop: producing the survey data that
defines the problem, then shipping the curriculum sold into it.** On August 18, 2026 it released a
nationally representative survey of **1,017 U.S. teens aged 13-17** (fielded April–May 2026) finding
that **70% use AI for schoolwork** while only **30% have ever had a teacher discuss safe use** and
26% how to evaluate AI accuracy; 63% report going straight to a chatbot for an answer; **38% say AI
availability leads them to develop fewer original ideas** and 39% feel they are missing out on
learning. Of the 44% who hit a school block on an AI tool, **59% switched to a personal device** with
less oversight. Alongside the findings it launched a **Digital Literacy & Well-Being Curriculum**
(K-8 and high school courses) and an **AI and Student Well-Being professional development course**,
and it runs a Youth AI Safety Institute whose advisory board includes former Education Secretary
John King. The organization's framing matters commercially: it is the most prominent source
reframing student AI use from an academic-integrity problem into a **wellbeing** problem, which is
the frame that unlocks wellbeing budgets rather than IT budgets.
*(as of 2026-W34)*
