## Current snapshot

**The FCC has opened the question of whether to end E-rate, and folded school screen-time politics
into a telecom proceeding.** Chair Brendan Carr's commission approved a notice of proposed
rulemaking on [[e-rate]] at its June 25, 2026 meeting; the proposed rule was published in the
Federal Register on **August 14, 2026**, and public comment is open until **October 13, 2026**. The
notice asks whether E-rate policy changes are warranted given screen-time concerns in schools and
whether the program "should be limited or sunset" in light of "today's extensive connectivity
rates." It also asks whether E-rate support should be conditioned on schools giving parents "a
meaningful opportunity to opt their children out of screen-based instruction or screen use during
the school day" — which would make the FCC, not ED, the agency setting a federal screen-time
condition on schools. That makes the FCC a newly relevant regulator for the vault: it controls the
fifth-largest federal funding stream reaching districts, and it is now the third venue (alongside ED
guidance and state legislatures) where the [[screen-time-device-policy]] question is being decided.
*(as of 2026-W34)*
