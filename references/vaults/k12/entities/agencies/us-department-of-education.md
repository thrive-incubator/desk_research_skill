## Current snapshot

**ED spent W34 setting procurement and enforcement expectations by letter rather than by rule — and
in both cases the instrument was a Dear Colleague Letter with no force of regulation.** On August 20
Assistant Secretary of Elementary and Secondary Education **Kirsten Baesler** issued edtech guidance
telling states and districts to make technology use "purposeful, instructionally sound, and
contributing to measurable student outcomes," that "responsible design is the floor," and — the
commercially decisive instruction — to **incorporate evidence of effectiveness into procurement,
especially products that have conducted randomized controlled trials**. It asks districts to
distinguish "recreational" from "instructional" technology and to stop evaluating tools by screen
time alone, asks vendors to publish independent evaluations "whenever feasible," and **praises
Arkansas, Indiana, Louisiana, Michigan and Texas for contracting models based on performance
measures rather than adoption or usage metrics** — a federal endorsement of
[[outcomes-based-contracting]] by description if not by name. Notably it declined to issue rules,
expressly delegating implementation to states and districts. Two days earlier, Assistant Secretary
for Civil Rights **Kimberly Richey** issued discipline guidance stating that **"it is race
discrimination under Title VI for the school to evaluate the racial outcomes of their discipline
policies and to make decisions based on or because of those racial outcomes"** — following the
administration's formal undoing of the disparate-impact theory the prior month, and inverting the
compliance driver that sold discipline analytics. Carried forward from W32 and still live: the
temporary restraining order preserving the school mental-health grants **runs only through August
24, 2026**, and the June 2026 interagency agreement moving IDEA Parts B, C and D grant
administration to [[hhs]] remains a completed transfer ED is defending rather than proposing.
*(as of 2026-W34)*

### 2026-W34 — ED edtech guidance tells districts to put RCT evidence into procurement, declines to write rules
