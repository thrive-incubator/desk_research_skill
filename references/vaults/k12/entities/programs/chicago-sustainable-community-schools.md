## Current snapshot

Chicago Public Schools' Sustainable Community Schools program, launched in **2018**, pays schools
**$500,000 annually** to work with community-based organizations on wraparound, academic and social
support — including hiring additional mental-health professionals. In W32 it expanded by 15 schools
to **51 campuses**, with district spending rising to approximately **$38.6 million** from $24.6
million. The **Chicago Teachers Union contract requires 70 schools by its 2028 expiration**, which
is the single most important fact about this program: its growth is contractually obligated, not
discretionary.

That makes SCS the vault's clearest case study in **funding durability**. In the same 2026-27 budget,
CPS halved Whole School Safety Plan funding — from $8.7M to $4M across 54 high schools, cutting
funded positions from 80 to 42 — because that money carried the label "temporary transition
funding." Same district, same budget, opposite directions, decided by funding classification rather
than evidence or popularity (see [[school-mental-health-funding]]).

One caveat on execution: a prior Chalkbeat analysis found mixed outcomes, with schools leaving
**$6.6 million unspent** and chronic absenteeism higher despite improved graduation rates. At
$500,000 per school the binding constraint is not money but the capacity to deploy it — which is
where an implementation-side product or partner has an opening.
*(as of 2026-W32)*

## Timeline

### 2026-W32 — Program expands to 51 schools; spending rises to $38.6M
*Chalkbeat · August 3, 2026* · [link](https://www.chalkbeat.org/chicago/2026/08/03/fifteen-more-schools-join-sustainable-community-schools-program/)
Fifteen more schools joined for the upcoming school year, bringing the total to 51. Each receives
$500,000 annually to work with community-based organizations on extra programming and support for
students and parents, including hiring more mental-health professionals; Mayor Brandon Johnson
described it as wraparound, academic and social support. District spending rises to roughly $38.6M
from $24.6M. The program launched in 2018, and the CPS contract with the Chicago Teachers Union
requires 70 schools by the contract's 2028 expiration. Prior Chalkbeat analysis found mixed
outcomes, with $6.6M left unspent and higher chronic absenteeism despite improved graduation rates.
Of Chicago's roughly 630 public schools, 520 are district-run.

### 2026-W32 — Same CPS budget halves restorative-justice safety funding at 54 high schools
*Chalkbeat · July 30, 2026* · [link](https://www.chalkbeat.org/chicago/2026/07/30/cps-to-cut-money-staff-for-schools-removed-police-officers/)
The proposed 2026-27 budget cuts Whole School Safety Plan funding for 54 high schools from $8.7M to
$4M, with most schools dropping from $160,000 or more to roughly $80,000 and funded positions
falling from 80 to 42. The money paid for climate and culture coordinators, restorative justice
coordinators, social workers and security guards at schools that removed police officers, beginning
with school-level votes in 2020 and the board's 2024 vote to end the district police contract. CPS
characterized the cut as a planned adjustment to "temporary transition funding" while introducing a
citywide framework to expand restorative justice practices districtwide.
