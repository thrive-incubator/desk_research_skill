## Current snapshot

**E-rate is the fifth-largest federal funding stream reaching school districts, and it is under
active review for possible sunset.** Created by the bipartisan Telecommunications Act signed in
1996, the program discounts internet and telecommunications services for schools and libraries. It
delivered **$10.5 billion to school districts between 2021 and 2025**, and **$13.2 billion in total
to nearly 25,000 entities** — districts, schools, consortiums and libraries — over the same period,
with the majority going to districts. The [[fcc]] published a proposed rule on August 14, 2026
asking whether the program should be "limited or sunset" and whether support should be conditioned
on parental opt-outs from screen-based instruction; comment closes **October 13, 2026**. The
Education and Libraries Network Coalition (EdLiNC), co-chaired by AASA, the American Library
Association and the Bernstein Strategy Group, is organizing opposition and argues the program covers
only eligible services and equipment — not devices or content — so curriculum and screen-use
questions should be settled at state and local level. AASA planned a virtual town hall for
September 9, 2026. Program history for scale: 8% of public school classrooms had internet access in
1995, rising to 93% by 2003.
*(as of 2026-W34)*

## Timeline

### 2026-W34 — FCC opens comment on whether to limit or sunset E-rate; $10.5B to districts 2021–2025
*K-12 Dive · August 18, 2026* · [link](https://www.k12dive.com/news/fcc-opens-comment-period-on-e-rates-future/828069/)
The FCC's proposed rule, published in the Federal Register August 14, asks whether E-rate "should be limited or sunset" and whether support should require parental opt-outs from screen-based instruction; comment closes October 13, 2026. E-rate accounted for $10.5 billion to districts between 2021 and 2025 and $13.2 billion in total to nearly 25,000 entities, making it the fifth-largest pot of federal funds schools receive. EdLiNC, co-chaired by AASA, called on the commission to strengthen rather than terminate the program (also: [The 74, August 19, 2026](https://www.the74million.org/article/fcc-review-could-end-school-internet-program-advocates-plan-very-loud-protest/)).
