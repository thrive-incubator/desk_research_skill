## Current snapshot

The federal tax-credit scholarship program is set to launch **January 1, 2027** with
near-universal eligibility: an American Federation for Children analysis puts **51.7 million of
56.4 million students** (about 9 in 10) within scope, covering families up to 300% of area median
income — a threshold the report describes as ranging from $234,000 to nearly $500,000 for a family
of four. Actual reach depends on state opt-in, which is still in motion: **31 states** have opted
in or signaled intent and **19 states plus D.C.** have not, leaving 30.9 million eligible students
(about 60%) in participating states. California has not opted in. Eligibility is not receipt —
state decisions and scholarship-granting capacity still gate the flow — but the design makes this
a parallel funding rail to district budgets rather than a targeted program, and any wellbeing
services purchased through it would sit outside district accountability structures.
*(as of 2026-W31)*

### 2026-W31 — Analysis puts 9 in 10 students in scope ahead of the January 1, 2027 launch
*K-12 Dive · July 28, 2026* · [link](https://www.k12dive.com/news/9-in-10-students-expected-to-be-eligible-for-education-freedom-tax-credit/826295/)
An American Federation for Children analysis released July 28, authored by senior fellow Patrick
Graff, estimates 51.7M of 56.4M students will be eligible when the credit launches January 1,
2027 — families up to 300% of area median income, put at $234,000 to nearly $500,000 for a family
of four. 31 states have opted in or signaled intent; 19 states plus D.C. have not, leaving 30.9M
eligible students (about 60%) in participating states, with eligibility rates ranging from 86%
(D.C.) to 96% (Maine). Graff notes "Eligibility this broad is a design feature of the statute"
(also: [The 74, July 28](https://www.the74million.org/article/52-million-u-s-kids-eligible-for-new-scholarships-but-california-hasnt-opted-in/)).
