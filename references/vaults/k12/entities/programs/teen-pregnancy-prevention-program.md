## Current snapshot

The Teen Pregnancy Prevention Program is a **~$101 million/year [[hhs]] grant program**, established
in 2010, that has reached over **1.4 million youth**. It is now largely dismantled: **all but 14 of
its grants have been terminated** as HHS shifts toward abstinence-based initiatives. (Source
denominators differ slightly — W32 reporting described 53 of 66 grants canceled on June 26, 2026;
W34 reporting describes 67 grants nationwide with all but 14 terminated. Treat the count as "roughly
53 of ~66-67 terminated.") The cancellations followed new funding requirements mandating
"abstinence-only sexual risk avoidance" education, alignment with agency priorities prohibiting
"gender ideology" and DEI practices, and restrictions on discussing teen sexual activity; HHS claimed
some curricula were "normalizing adolescent sexual activity." A July 2026 lawsuit challenges the
changes as violating the program's statutory mandate to use evidence-based, medically accurate
interventions.

W34 shows what the terminations look like at the delivery layer. **Three New York grantees are losing
over $4.3 million a year** that funded evidence-based sexual health education for **over 19,000 young
people annually**: **Children's Aid** (a $936,700 annual grant terminated effective immediately two
years before expiry, HHS citing lack of alignment with its priorities; it faces eliminating most
programming including peer mentors and sex ed at the city's Children's Center foster-care intake
facility), **NYC Teens Connection** (losing $3.9M over two years with 15 staff at risk; it has worked
with over 400 organizations and served nearly 130,000 teenagers since 2010), and **HOPE Buffalo** run
by Cicatelli Associates ($1.4M annually; credited with a 36% decline in teen pregnancy rates in
high-poverty Buffalo neighborhoods from 2015 to 2020). Earlier, Philadelphia's AccessMatters lost a
five-year grant with $2.4M remaining while serving 2,200+ youth across nine schools. Context that
sharpens the cut: teen pregnancy rates in New York state and New York City **rose between 2021 and
2023, the first increase in two decades**. New York's congressional delegation wrote to Secretary
Kennedy urging reinstatement.

The program's importance to this vault is less its size than its **mechanism**: requirements
rewritten first, cancellations for non-compliance second — redirecting a categorical youth-health
stream without touching the appropriation or requiring Congress, and portable to any grant program
with content standards. W34 adds the sharpest available counterexample to the "prove outcomes and the
money follows" thesis: HOPE Buffalo had a documented 36% outcome improvement and was terminated on
priority alignment anyway.
*(as of 2026-W34)*

## Timeline

### 2026-W34 — Terminations reach named New York grantees: $4.3M/year, 19,000 young people
*Chalkbeat / New York Focus · August 14, 2026* · [link](https://www.chalkbeat.org/newyork/2026/08/14/trump-administration-cuts-sex-education-funding/)
Of 67 federal TPP grants nationwide, all but 14 have been terminated. Three New York grantees are losing over $4.3 million in annual funding serving over 19,000 young people: Children's Aid ($936,700/year, terminated immediately two years early, HHS citing lack of alignment with its priorities), NYC Teens Connection ($3.9M over two years, 15 staff at risk, nearly 130,000 teens served since 2010), and HOPE Buffalo run by Cicatelli Associates ($1.4M/year, credited with a 36% decline in teen pregnancy rates in high-poverty Buffalo neighborhoods 2015-2020). Teen pregnancy rates in New York state and city rose between 2021 and 2023, the first increase in two decades; the state's congressional delegation asked HHS Secretary Kennedy to reinstate funding.

### 2026-W32 — 53 of 66 grants canceled after HHS rewrote the content requirements
*K-12 Dive · August 5, 2026* · [link](https://www.k12dive.com/news/trump-administration-targets-teen-pregnancy-prevention-grants/827001/)
HHS canceled 53 of 66 Teen Pregnancy Prevention Program grants on June 26, 2026, three days after
releasing new funding requirements mandating "abstinence-only sexual risk avoidance" education,
alignment with agency priorities prohibiting "gender ideology" and DEI practices, and restrictions
on discussions of teen sexual activity. The ~$101M/year program has reached over 1.4 million youth
since its 2010 establishment. HHS's cancellation notice claimed some curricula were "normalizing
adolescent sexual activity" and contained "overtly sexually explicit or pornographic content."
Philadelphia's AccessMatters lost a five-year grant with $2.4M remaining, having served 2,200+ youth
across nine schools with HIV/STI prevention, pregnancy prevention and healthy-relationships
curricula. A July lawsuit in U.S. District Court argues the policy changes violate the program's
statutory mandate to use evidence-based, medically accurate interventions.
