This is the single most consequential item for wellbeing funding this week.
- **The same week, the enforcement layer around those students narrowed.** ED rescinded its Title VI disparate-impact regulations and limited civil-rights investigations into racial disparities ([K-12 Dive, Jul 24](https://www.k12dive.com/news/education-department-rescinds-disparate-impact-regulations/826188/)). Grant terminations remove the money; the disparate-impact rescission removes a mechanism families used to contest what happens after.
- **The growth money in the K12 stack is no longer appropriated money.** Two channels expanded this week, and neither runs through an ED grant program: the Education Freedom Tax Credit, which an advocacy analysis puts at 51.7M of 56.4M students eligible when it launches January 1, 2027 ([K-12 Dive, Jul 28](https://www.k12dive.com/news/9-in-10-students-expected-to-be-eligible-for-education-freedom-tax-credit/826295/)), and social-media tort settlements, where one Kentucky district banked $27M against a $22M annual budget ([K-12 Dive, Jul 28](https://www.k12dive.com/news/how-school-districts-are-preparing-for-social-media-lawsuit-payouts/824384/)). For a wellbeing idea trying to find a buyer, the settlement channel is the more interesting one — it is explicitly being organized around youth wellbeing programming, and it is district-controlled.
- **Districts are still buying wellbeing capacity inside deficits, selectively.** Chicago is adding hundreds of special-education positions while closing a $732.5M deficit — and cutting counseling positions at other schools in the same move ([Chalkbeat, Jul 28](https://www.chalkbeat.org/chicago/2026/07/28/cps-schools-to-lose-staff-for-next-school-year-budget-deficit-looming/)). Memphis moved all 69 attendance liaisons to full-time at its highest-absenteeism schools ([Chalkbeat, Jul 23](https://www.chalkbeat.org/tennessee/2026/07/23/memphis-schools-focuses-on-chronic-absenteeism-amid-state-takeover/)). Both are staffing decisions, not procurement — the purchasing signal stays invisible to this instrument.
- **And the evidence base moved under the field's feet.** Ron Avi Astor, whose work underwrote a decade of SEL, restorative-justice, and trauma-informed spending as school-shooting prevention, publicly retracted the causal claim ([Hechinger, Jul 27](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-school-shootings/)). The interventions may still be worth funding; the *rationale* that sold many of them to boards no longer holds. Expect this to surface in procurement justifications within a year.

### Education Department rescinds disparate impact regulations
*K-12 Dive · July 24, 2026* · [link](https://www.k12dive.com/news/education-department-rescinds-disparate-impact-regulations/826188/) (also: [Chalkbeat, Jul 23](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2026/07/23/department-of-education-limits-civil-rights-investigations/))

Announced Thursday and published in the Federal Register Friday, the Office for Civil Rights eliminated enforcement of disparate-impact provisions under Title VI, which had been used to investigate districts over racial discrimination. ED argued the provision sits in "considerable tension" with Title VI and the equal protection clause, following the April 2025 executive order directing elimination of disparate-impact liability. Future investigations are expected to require a showing of intentional discrimination.

**So what:** Disparate impact was the theory behind discipline-disparity and access-to-services complaints — the cases that most often reached school mental-health and behavioral-support practice. Removing it narrows the federal lever without changing any dollar figure.

### 9 in 10 students expected to be eligible for Education Freedom Tax Credit
*K-12 Dive · July 28, 2026* · [link](https://www.k12dive.com/news/9-in-10-students-expected-to-be-eligible-for-education-freedom-tax-credit/826295/) (also: [The 74, Jul 28](https://www.the74million.org/article/52-million-u-s-kids-eligible-for-new-scholarships-but-california-hasnt-opted-in/))

An American Federation for Children analysis released July 28 estimates 51.7M of 56.4M students will be eligible when the credit launches January 1, 2027 — families up to 300% of area median income, which the report puts at $234,000 to nearly $500,000 for a family of four. 31 states have opted in or signaled intent; 19 states plus D.C. have not, leaving 30.9M eligible students (about 60%) in participating states. California has not opted in.

**So what:** Eligibility is not receipt — state opt-in and scholarship-granting-organization capacity still gate the actual flow. But a near-universal eligibility design means this becomes a parallel funding rail to district budgets rather than a targeted program, and wellbeing services purchased through it would sit outside district accountability structures entirely.

### Facing state scrutiny, Memphis schools intensify efforts to reduce chronic absenteeism
*Chalkbeat Tennessee · July 23, 2026* · [link](https://www.chalkbeat.org/tennessee/2026/07/23/memphis-schools-focuses-on-chronic-absenteeism-amid-state-takeover/)

**So what:** The clearest live example of absenteeism functioning as the accountability frame that unlocks wellbeing spending. Note what the liaisons actually deliver — transport, clothing, food, mental-health referral — which is case management funded as attendance work because attendance is what the state measures.

## District Purchasing Layer

### Social media lawsuits could net payouts. How are districts planning?
*K-12 Dive · July 28, 2026* · [link](https://www.k12dive.com/news/how-school-districts-are-preparing-for-social-media-lawsuit-payouts/824384/)

Roughly 1,400 districts have sued Meta, Snap, YouTube, and TikTok. Kentucky's Breathitt County Schools settled for $27M in May 2026 — more than its $22M annual budget. Six bellwether cases are being tracked; the next trials, Tucson Unified (AZ) and Charleston County (SC), are set for February 8, 2027. The Children's Funding Project's "Fund In Real Life" initiative is organizing districts to direct payouts toward child-wellbeing activities, calling them "once-in-a-generation funding." Spokane Public Schools has pre-invested in its "Engage in Real Life" program — $600K in FY2024-25, $500K in 2025-26 — running 284 after-school activities with 65% student participation and a 75% target by 2028.

**So what:** This is the most important item in the digest for a wellbeing organization. It is a large, growing, district-controlled funding pool with no federal strings, an explicit wellbeing purpose, and an organized intermediary already shaping how it gets spent — arriving exactly as federal wellbeing grants contract. The Spokane model (structured real-world activity, measured participation) is also the intervention type being funded, which is a different bet than screening-and-referral software.

## Ideas & Influence Layer

### A leading school shootings researcher says he was wrong
*Hechinger Report · July 27, 2026* · [link](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-school-shootings/)

Ron Avi Astor (UCLA), a leading school-violence scholar, retracted decades of his own position at the April 2026 AERA meeting: "We were wrong." His theory held that shooters were driven by local grievances — bullying, trauma, school climate — and that school-based intervention could therefore prevent shootings, a claim that drove heavy district investment in SEL, restorative justice, and trauma-informed care. He now points to a divergence: weapon-carrying among California students fell steadily over 25 years while national school-shooting incidents rose. He argues perpetrators are seeking national notoriety, and recommends media and platform coverage guidelines rather than school-based prevention alone.

**So what:** The most consequential idea-layer event of the week. A large share of school wellbeing spending was sold to boards on violence prevention; the researcher who supplied that justification has withdrawn it. This does not make SEL or trauma-informed care ineffective at what they actually do — it removes the argument that they prevent shootings. Organizations funding wellbeing work should get ahead of this by re-anchoring their evidence claims on outcomes the research still supports, before a board member cites this reversal in a budget hearing.
