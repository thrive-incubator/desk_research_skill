# Thrive K12 Funding & Wellbeing Synthesis
### Week of August 6 – August 13, 2026

## This Week's Throughline

- **The evidence bar is finally biting vendors.** A major virtual tutoring provider shut down with experts pointing at thin efficacy evidence, in the same week Hechinger documented outcomes-based contracting spreading — districts paying vendors only when student results move. For wellbeing products, this cuts both ways: it raises the burden of proof, but it also creates a purchasing channel that rewards a product that can actually demonstrate outcomes.
- **Districts are cutting the things that sit closest to wellbeing.** Miami-Dade ended universal free meals, LAUSD is laying off thousands of workers, Chicago could only partially reverse its own layoffs and is weighing new revenue referendums. This is the post-ESSER structural squeeze showing up in the non-instructional staff and services layer — exactly where school mental health and student support live.
- **Federal action this week was deregulatory and health-side, not education-side.** ACF proposed reducing Head Start requirements (providers say it would gut the program's identity), and CMS issued a rule narrowing federal Medicaid/CHIP coverage for certain procedures for minors. Nothing new flowed *toward* school mental health from ED.
- **The screen-time fight is becoming a procurement fight.** CoSN publicly advised districts against blanket screen-time limits the same week LAUSD started a screen-free year for its youngest students. Whichever framing wins locally determines whether a digital wellbeing tool is a solution or part of the problem.
- **Blind spot to keep in view:** everything above about district *buying* is inferred from budget journalism, not observed procurement. We see cuts announced; we don't see the contracts.

## Federal Funding & Policy

### ACF proposes "Reducing Federal Burden for Head Start Programs"; providers say it would change what Head Start is
*Federal Register (HHS, Administration for Children and Families) · August 7, 2026* · [link](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/07/2026-16134/reducing-federal-burden-for-head-start-programs)

A proposed rule from the Administration for Children and Families would pare back federal requirements on Head Start programs. Providers pushed back immediately, with one telling Hechinger the result "wouldn't be Head Start anymore" (also: [Hechinger Report, August 8, 2026](https://hechingerreport.org/it-wouldnt-be-head-start-anymore-providers-blast-federal-proposal-to-alter-preschool-program/)).

**So what:** Head Start's performance standards are the floor that mandates comprehensive services — health, mental health consultation, family support. Loosening them removes the compliance driver that makes many early-childhood wellbeing services procurable in the first place. Comment period is the window to watch.

### CMS issues rule barring federal Medicaid and CHIP funding for certain procedures furnished to children
*Federal Register (HHS, Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services) · August 13, 2026* · [link](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/13/2026-16508/medicaid-program-prohibition-on-federal-medicaid-and-childrens-health-insurance-program-funding-for)

A final rule titled "Medicaid Program; Prohibition on Federal Medicaid and Children's Health Insurance Program Funding for Sex-Rejecting Procedures Furnished to Children."

**So what:** On its face this targets clinical procedures, not school-based services, so the school-Medicaid billing channel is untouched. It is worth logging as the direction of travel at CMS on federal child health coverage — the same agency that governs whether districts can bill Medicaid for school mental health.
