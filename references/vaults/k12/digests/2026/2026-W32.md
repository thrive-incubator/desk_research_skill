# Thrive K12 Funding & Wellbeing Synthesis
### Week of July 30 – August 6, 2026

## This Week's Throughline

- **Federal wellbeing money is being decided in court, not in Congress.** A federal judge blocked the Education Department from terminating school-based mental health grants — the second time in eight months the same 15-state coalition has had to litigate the same dollars. The temporary restraining order expires August 24. Simultaneously, HHS canceled 53 of 66 Teen Pregnancy Prevention grants ($101M/year), and IDEA Parts B, C and D grant administration quietly moved from ED to HHS under a June interagency agreement. The pattern: the *administrative* layer, not appropriations, is where youth-wellbeing dollars are now won and lost — and litigation is the control valve.
- **Chicago is the week's clearest lesson in which wellbeing money survives.** The same district expanded Sustainable Community Schools to 51 campuses ($38.6M, up from $24.6M) while halving Whole School Safety Plan funding from $8.7M to $4M across 54 high schools — cutting those positions from 80 to 42. The difference isn't evidence or popularity: the first is written into the CTU contract, the second was labeled "temporary transition funding." **For anyone building wellbeing products, that is the durability test** — funding written into statute or a bargained contract outlasts funding that carries a transitional label.
- **States remain the quiet, reliable channel.** Iowa's $2.35M for 19 new therapeutic classrooms is small money, but it is statutory (2020 law), in its sixth year, and recurring — the opposite risk profile from anything currently flowing through ED.
- **The most actionable structural signal is procurement, not policy.** New WestEd evidence found outcomes-based contracting improved student performance at comparable cost; completed contracts grew from 5 to 46 since 2022, with 87 school systems now participating. For wellbeing vendors that can actually measure outcomes, this is a purchasing model that rewards efficacy — the rare place where "it works" converts directly into "it gets bought."
- **Caveat on what we can see:** the district items below are budget *journalism*, not observed procurement. We infer demand from budget votes and layoff notices; we do not see RFPs or awards.

### Trump administration cancels most Teen Pregnancy Prevention grants
*K-12 Dive · August 5, 2026* · [link](https://www.k12dive.com/news/trump-administration-targets-teen-pregnancy-prevention-grants/827001/)

HHS canceled 53 of 66 grants under the ~$101 million/year Teen Pregnancy Prevention Program on June 26, 2026, three days after releasing new funding requirements mandating "abstinence-only sexual risk avoidance" content and alignment with agency priorities barring "gender ideology" and DEI practices. The program has reached more than 1.4 million youth since its 2010 establishment. One grantee, Philadelphia's AccessMatters, lost a five-year grant with $2.4 million remaining, having served 2,200+ youth across nine schools. A July lawsuit argues the changes violate the program's statutory mandate to use evidence-based, medically accurate interventions.

**So what:** Note the mechanism — the content requirements were changed first, then grants were canceled for non-compliance. That is a repeatable template for redirecting any categorical youth-health stream without touching the appropriation.

### IDEA grant administration moves from Education Department to HHS
*K-12 Dive · August 4, 2026* · [link](https://www.k12dive.com/news/education-department-promotes-special-education-shakeup-OSEP-conference/826981/)

Education Secretary Linda McMahon defended the reorganization at the OSEP conference on August 4. Under a June 2026 interagency agreement, the Office of Special Education and Rehabilitative Services moved formula and discretionary grant program components for IDEA Parts B, C and D to HHS, while ED retains legal responsibility for the underlying education law. ED now has 14 interagency agreements with six federal agencies. Special educators, disability rights advocates and some members of Congress warn the split creates confusion and reduces accountability for compliance.

**So what:** Part C (early intervention) and Part B (school-age special education) are major funding channels for school-based therapeutic and behavioral services. Splitting money administration from legal authority means vendors may soon answer to two agencies with different reporting expectations for a single funded service.

## District Purchasing Layer

### Chicago halves funding for schools that replaced police with restorative justice
*Chalkbeat · July 30, 2026* · [link](https://www.chalkbeat.org/chicago/2026/07/30/cps-to-cut-money-staff-for-schools-removed-police-officers/)

The proposed CPS 2026-27 budget cuts Whole School Safety Plan funding for 54 high schools from $8.7 million to $4 million. Most affected schools drop from $160,000 or more to roughly $80,000. Funded positions fall from 80 to 42 — about one lost position per school. The money paid for climate and culture coordinators, restorative justice coordinators, social workers and security guards at schools that removed police officers, a process that began with school-level votes in 2020 and culminated in the board's 2024 vote to end the district police contract. CPS characterized the reduction as a planned wind-down of "temporary transition funding" while introducing a districtwide restorative justice framework.

**So what:** The clearest observed case this week of wellbeing staffing being cut not on evidence but on funding classification. "Temporary transition funding" is a label with an expiry date attached, and districts honor the label. A vendor whose district champion is paid from a line with that label is on a clock.

### NYC overhauls how schools handle school avoidance under legal agreement
*Chalkbeat · August 3, 2026* · [link](https://www.chalkbeat.org/newyork/2026/08/03/nyc-lawsuit-agreement-creates-school-avoidance-guidance-liaisons/)

Under an agreement signed August 3 settling an October 2024 class action by the Legal Aid Society's Education Law Project and Pillsbury Winthrop Shaw Pittman on behalf of four students with disabilities, every NYC school must designate a school avoidance liaison annually by October 31 and provide two hours of training on behaviors linked to school refusal. The Education Department must produce training materials by January 1, 2027, covering parent conversations and gradual reintegration plans, and may no longer refuse special education evaluations because a student is not physically present. Compliance reporting is due July 31, 2027. No dollar figures were disclosed.

**So what:** School avoidance — chronic absence driven by anxiety and depression — just became a named, staffed, court-supervised function in the country's largest district, with a training-material deadline. Litigation, not appropriation, created the role. Watch whether other large districts adopt the structure preemptively.

## Ideas & Influence Layer

### Early evidence: outcomes-based contracting improves student performance
*K-12 Dive · August 4, 2026* · [link](https://www.k12dive.com/news/early-evidence-finds-outcomes-based-contracting-boosts-student-performance/826856/)

WestEd, with the Center for Outcomes Based Contracting, studied eight districts across California, Florida, Mississippi and Texas over the 2022–25 school years, interviewing seven district leaders, six tutoring providers, three edtech vendors and 12 school leaders. Three districts showed positive causal impacts on student performance, including large second-grade proficiency gains on an AI-based ELA platform and substantial increases in students hitting typical reading growth targets. Districts spent comparable amounts to traditional contracts, suggesting greater service intensity per dollar. Completed outcomes-based contracts grew from 5 to 46 between 2022 and 2025, with 87 school systems now participating. (also: [The 74 opinion, August 6](https://www.the74million.org/article/accountability-works-outcomes-based-contracting-makes-it-work-better/))

**So what:** The most important item in this section for Thrive's purposes. Outcomes-based contracting is a procurement mechanism that pays for demonstrated results — structurally advantageous for wellbeing interventions with real evidence and structurally hostile to those without. The measurement problem becomes the go-to-market problem. Anyone building here should be able to name their outcome metric and who validates it.
